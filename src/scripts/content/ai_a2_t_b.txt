# A2 travel, places, leisure: sentence-choice (typical mistakes), word-order, odd-one-out
sc | T+PREP | We arrived in London at six o'clock in the evening. | We arrived to London at six o'clock in the evening. :: We say "arrive in" a city, not "arrive to". | We arrived at London at six o'clock in the evening. :: For a big city we say "arrive in", not "arrive at". | The correct phrase is "arrive in a city".
sc | T+PREP | I'm interested in learning about the history of the city. | I'm interested on learning about the history of the city. :: We say "interested in", not "interested on". | I'm interested to learning about the history of the city. :: We say "interested in" + -ing. | We say "interested in".
sc | T+PREP | We stayed at a small hotel near the sea. | We stayed in a small hotel near to sea. :: "near the sea" needs "the". | We stayed at a small hotel near of the sea. :: We say "near the sea", without "of". | We say "near the sea".
sc | T+ART | We need some information about the trains. | We need an information about the trains. :: "information" is uncountable, so we cannot use "an". | We need some informations about the trains. :: "information" has no plural. | Use "some information" or "a piece of information".
sc | T+ART | Our luggage is heavy. | Our luggages are heavy. :: "luggage" is uncountable and has no plural. | Our luggage are heavy. :: "luggage" is singular, so we use "is". | "Luggage" is uncountable.
sc | T+VT | We went to the beach yesterday and swam in the sea. | We go to the beach yesterday and swam in the sea. :: "yesterday" needs the past simple: went. | We went to the beach yesterday and swum in the sea. :: The past simple of "swim" is "swam", not "swum". | Use past simple forms with "yesterday".
sc | T+VT | Last weekend we visited a beautiful castle. | Last weekend we visit a beautiful castle. :: "Last weekend" is past, so we need the past simple: visited. | Last weekend we were visit a beautiful castle. :: "were visit" is not a correct form. | Use the past simple with "last weekend".
sc | T+VT | I didn't see the museum because it was closed. | I didn't saw the museum because it was closed. :: After "didn't" we use the base form: see. | I don't saw the museum because it was closed. :: The past negative is "didn't + base form". | After "didn't" use the base form.
sc | T+ADJ | This hotel is cheaper than the other one. | This hotel is more cheap than the other one. :: With short adjectives we add -er: cheaper. | This hotel is cheaper that the other one. :: After a comparative we use "than". | Use "cheaper than".
sc | T+ADJ | It is the most beautiful city in Italy. | It is the more beautiful city in Italy. :: For three or more things we use the superlative: the most beautiful. | It is the most beautifulest city in Italy. :: We do not use "most" and "-est" together. | Use "the most + long adjective".
sc | T+ADJ | The train is faster than the bus. | The train is more fast than the bus. :: "fast" is short, so the comparative is "faster". | The train is fastest than the bus. :: "-est" is a superlative; for two things we use the comparative. | Short adjectives add -er.
sc | T+GER | I enjoy travelling by train. | I enjoy to travel by train. :: After "enjoy" we use the -ing form. | I enjoy travel by train. :: After "enjoy" we need the -ing form: travelling. | Enjoy is followed by -ing.
sc | T+GER | We decided to go to Greece. | We decided going to Greece. :: After "decide" we use "to + verb". | We decided go to Greece. :: After "decide" we need "to": decided to go. | "Decide" is followed by "to + verb".
sc | T+MOD | You mustn't use your phone on the plane. | You don't must use your phone on the plane. :: "must" does not use "don't"; the negative is "mustn't". | You mustn't to use your phone on the plane. :: After "mustn't" we use the base form without "to". | The negative of "must" is "mustn't".
sc | T+MOD | You can pay by card or in cash. | You can to pay by card or in cash. :: After "can" we use the base form without "to". | You can paying by card or in cash. :: After "can" we use the base form: pay. | After "can" use the base form.
sc | T+PRON | Let's take a taxi. It will take us there faster. | Let's take a taxi. It will take we there faster. :: After a verb we use the object pronoun "us". | Let's take a taxi. It will take our there faster. :: "our" is a possessive adjective and needs a noun. | After a verb we use "us".
sc | T+PRON | I love this city. I want to visit it again. | I love this city. I want to visit he again. :: A city is "it", not "he". | I love this city. I want to visit its again. :: "its" is a possessive and needs a noun. | A city is called "it".
sc | T+ART | There aren't any seats left on the train. | There aren't some seats left on the train. :: In negative sentences we use "any", not "some". | There isn't any seats left on the train. :: "seats" is plural, so we use "aren't". | In negatives use "any".
sc | T+ART | How much does the ticket cost? | How many does the ticket cost? :: "many" is used with countable nouns; here we ask about the price. | How much costs the ticket? :: In questions with "do/does" the word order is: How much does the ticket cost? | The question is "How much does ... cost?".
sc | T+CONF | We have to wait for the bus. | We have to wait the bus. :: We say "wait for" something or someone. | We have to wait at the bus. :: "wait at the bus" means a different thing; we say "wait for the bus". | We say "wait for the bus".
sc | T+CONF | My friend lent me his guidebook. | My friend borrowed me his guidebook. :: "borrow" means to take something from someone; "lend" means to give it for a time. | My friend lend me his guidebook. :: The past simple of "lend" is "lent". | He gave it to me for a time, so we say "lent".
sc | T+CONF | The film was boring, so we left the cinema early. | The film was bored, so we left the cinema early. :: We use "boring" for the thing, and "bored" for the person. | The film was bore, so we left the cinema early. :: "bore" is a verb; the adjective is "boring". | "Boring" describes the film.
sc | T+ADJ | I'm very tired after the long walk. | I'm very tiring after the long walk. :: "tiring" describes something that makes you tired. | I'm very tire after the long walk. :: The adjective is "tired". | "Tired" describes how you feel.
