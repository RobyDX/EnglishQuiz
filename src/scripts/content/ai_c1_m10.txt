vf | VT | This is the second time this week that the system ___ down. | has gone | went :: "The second time this week" takes the present perfect. | had gone :: The past perfect does not fit. | goes :: The present simple does not fit. | "This is the second time" is followed by the present perfect.
vf | MOD | You ___ have mentioned it before; now we have no time to prepare. | could | can :: "Can have mentioned" does not express a missed chance. | will :: "Will have" does not fit. | may to :: "May to" is ungrammatical. | "Could have" expresses a missed chance.
vf | PRON | The two sisters, ___ of whom live abroad, visit us every summer. | both | either :: "Either of whom" changes the meaning. | all :: "All of whom" refers to more than two. | neither :: "Neither of whom live" has a negative sense. | "Both of whom" refers to two.
vc | VT | The children ___ (play) in the garden since lunch and are filthy. | have been playing | played :: The simple past does not link to now. | had been playing :: The past perfect does not fit. | are playing :: The present continuous does not show duration. | The present perfect continuous links duration to now.
vc | MOD | He ___ (may / leave) the building before the alarm sounded. | may have left | may leave :: The sentence refers to a past possibility. | must leave :: "Must leave" is an obligation. | can have left :: "Can have" is not used for possibility. | "May have" + participle expresses a past possibility.
vc | PRON | The woman ___ (whom / who) the police arrested has been released. | whom | who's :: "Who's" means "who is". | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | "Whom" is the object of "arrested".
vc | PREP | She has been addicted ___ (to / with) coffee for years. | to | with :: "Addicted with" is not standard. | of :: "Addicted of" is not standard. | by :: "Addicted by" is not standard. | "Addicted to" is the standard collocation.
es | MOD | She [shouldn't] to have gone alone. | shouldn't have | A modal is followed by "have" + participle.
es | MOD | He [mustn't] have failed; he is brilliant. | can't | "Can't have" expresses a negative deduction.
es | ART | Could you give me [an] advice? | some | "Advice" is uncountable.
es | ART | He has [many] time to spare today. | much | "Time" is uncountable.

es | PREP | He is fond [to] classical music. | of | "Fond of" is the standard collocation.
es | PREP | We agreed [for] the proposal. | to | "Agree to" is the standard collocation.
es | CONF | He [learned] me how to swim. | taught | "Teach" takes an object.
es | COND | If I [would] have more time, I would join a club. | had | The second conditional uses the past simple.
es | PREP | He was punished [of] cheating in the exam. | for | "Punished for" is the standard collocation.
es | CONF | Both of the workers [has] finished their tasks. | have | "Both" takes a plural verb.
