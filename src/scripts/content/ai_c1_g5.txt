# C1 batch 5: vocabulary in context (E, T, W, S) with mc/fb/vf/vc/ec/es
mc | E | She was left feeling thoroughly ___ after the interview, convinced that she had made a terrible impression. | deflated | deflected :: "Deflected" means turned aside. | deflowered :: This word has a different meaning. | defeated :: "Defeated" means beaten in a contest. | "Deflated" means suddenly lacking confidence.
mc | E | After weeks of ___ negotiations, the two families finally agreed on the terms of the sale. | protracted | protected :: "Protected" means kept safe. | protruded :: "Protruded" means stuck out. | protested :: "Protested" means objected. | "Protracted" means lasting longer than expected.
mc | E | The children's laughter echoed ___ the empty corridors of the old school. | through | across :: "Across" suggests a surface. | beyond :: "Beyond" suggests further than. | within :: "Within" suggests inside limits. | "Through" suggests passing along the length of a space.
mc | E | She seemed ___ to the noise and carried on reading as though nothing were happening. | oblivious | obvious :: "Obvious" means easy to see. | oblique :: "Oblique" means slanting. | obliged :: "Obliged" means bound by duty. | "Oblivious to" means unaware of.
mc | E | His ___ remarks about her appearance left her feeling deeply uncomfortable. | tactless | tactful :: "Tactful" means the opposite. | tactile :: "Tactile" relates to touch. | tactical :: "Tactical" relates to strategy. | "Tactless" means lacking sensitivity.
mc | E | After the funeral, the family gathered to ___ memories of the old man. | share | spare :: "Spare" means to give up. | shear :: "Shear" means to cut wool. | shard :: A "shard" is a broken piece. | "Share memories" means to exchange them.
mc | E | The old couple have been ___ for sixty years, and still hold hands when they walk. | devoted | devout :: "Devout" means deeply religious. | devoured :: "Devoured" means eaten greedily. | deflated :: "Deflated" means let down. | "Devoted" means loving and loyal.
mc | T | The mountain village is cut ___ for weeks every winter when the pass is blocked by snow. | off | out :: "Cut out" means to remove or stop. | down :: "Cut down" means to fell or reduce. | up :: "Cut up" means to divide. | "Cut off" means isolated.
mc | T | The tour group was ushered ___ the cathedral by an enthusiastic guide. | around | across :: "Across the cathedral" suggests a crossing. | over :: "Over the cathedral" suggests above. | behind :: "Behind the cathedral" suggests position. | "Around" suggests a tour of the interior.
mc | T | They spent a week ___ the coast in a converted fishing boat. | cruising along | cruising across :: "Across the coast" is not natural. | cruising through :: "Through the coast" is not natural. | cruising between :: "Between the coast" is not natural. | "Cruise along" means to travel at a leisurely pace.
mc | T | The village has managed to ___ its traditional character despite the arrival of mass tourism. | retain | restrain :: "Restrain" means to hold back. | retort :: "Retort" means to reply sharply. | retire :: "Retire" means to stop working. | "Retain" means to keep.
mc | W | The firm's decision to relocate abroad ___ a storm of protest from the workforce. | provoked | proved :: "Proved" means demonstrated. | provided :: "Provided" means supplied. | prohibited :: "Prohibited" means banned. | "Provoke" means to cause a strong reaction.
mc | W | The consultant's report was ___ of the management's handling of the crisis. | critical | critic :: A "critic" is a person. | criticism :: "Criticism" is a noun. | critically :: "Critically" is an adverb. | "Critical of" means expressing disapproval.
mc | W | After the scandal, the chairman was forced to ___ down from his position. | step | pull :: "Pull down" means to demolish. | walk :: "Walk down" is literal. | cut :: "Cut down" means to fell. | "Step down" means to resign.
mc | W | We need to ___ more resources to the project if it is to be completed on time. | allocate | allegiance :: "Allegiance" is loyalty. | alleviate :: "Alleviate" means to reduce suffering. | allude :: "Allude" means to refer to indirectly. | "Allocate" means to assign.
mc | S | The campaign aims to ___ awareness of the dangers of drinking and driving. | raise | rise :: "Rise" does not take an object. | arise :: "Arise" means to appear. | arouse :: "Arouse" means to stir feelings. | "Raise awareness" is the standard collocation.
mc | S | Many rural communities feel ___ by the government, which they accuse of ignoring their needs. | neglected | negligent :: "Negligent" describes a person who fails in a duty. | neglectful :: "Neglectful" describes the person who ignores. | negligible :: "Negligible" means very small. | "Neglected" describes how the communities feel.
fb | E | She was struck ___ the realisation that she had been wrong about him all along. | by | with :: "Struck with" is possible for fear or horror. | at :: "Struck at" means attacked. | on :: "Struck on" means impressed. | "Struck by" introduces the cause of a sudden impression.
fb | E | I've never been able to ___ the idea that something terrible is about to happen. | shake off | shake up :: "Shake up" means to disturb or reorganise. | shake out :: "Shake out" means to empty. | shake down :: "Shake down" means to extort. | "Shake off" means to get rid of.
fb | E | Having been brought up in a strict household, she found it hard to ___ her inhibitions. | shed | shred :: "Shred" means to cut into strips. | shell :: "Shell" means to remove from a case. | shine :: "Shine" means to give light. | "Shed inhibitions" means to lose them.
fb | E | The two friends ___ out over a trivial matter and did not speak for years. | fell | fall :: A past form is needed. | fallen :: An auxiliary is missing. | felt :: "Felt out" is not a phrasal verb here. | "Fall out" means to quarrel.
fb | T | The holiday brochure described the hotel as ___ in the heart of the old town. | nestled | nestle :: A participle is needed. | nested :: "Nested" means made a nest. | nettled :: "Nettled" means irritated. | "Nestled" means situated snugly.
fb | T | It was a ___ journey, taking us through deserts, mountains and dense forests. | gruelling | grilling :: "Grilling" means cooking or questioning. | growling :: "Growling" is a sound. | grudging :: "Grudging" means reluctant. | "Gruelling" means exhausting.
fb | W | She decided to ___ her career in the civil service and become a full-time writer. | abandon | abundant :: "Abundant" is an adjective. | abandoned :: After "to" use the base form. | abandonment :: "Abandonment" is a noun. | "Abandon" means to give up completely.
fb | W | The two firms agreed to ___ forces in order to compete with the larger multinationals. | join | joint :: "Joint" is an adjective. | jointed :: "Jointed" is a participle. | joined :: After "to" use the base form. | "Join forces" means to combine efforts.
vf | E | I would never have guessed that he ___ so shy as a child. | had been | was :: The past perfect shows an earlier state. | has been :: The present perfect does not fit. | is :: A present tense does not fit. | The past perfect shows a state before the time of guessing.
vf | T | We ___ the summit by dusk if the weather had not turned against us. | would have reached | would reach :: This refers to the present. | had reached :: The result clause needs a modal. | will have reached :: "Will" does not fit an unreal past. | The third conditional result uses "would have" + participle.
vf | W | It is high time the company ___ its recruitment procedures. | reviewed | reviews :: After "it is high time" a past form is used. | will review :: "Will" is not used here. | has reviewed :: The present perfect does not fit. | "High time" + past simple states that action is overdue.
vf | S | No sooner ___ the new law come into force than it was challenged in the courts. | had | has :: The past perfect is needed. | did :: "Did the new law come" lacks the correct pair. | was :: "Was the new law come" is ungrammatical. | "No sooner had" is the standard inversion.
vc | T | Hardly ___ (the train / leave) the station when the storm broke. | had the train left | the train had left :: "Hardly" at the start requires inversion. | did the train leave :: "Hardly did ... when" is not the standard pair. | left the train :: The auxiliary is missing. | "Hardly had ... when" is the standard correlative pair.
vc | S | If more people ___ (vaccinate) earlier, the epidemic would not have spread so far. | had been vaccinated | were vaccinated :: The third conditional needs the past perfect. | would be vaccinated :: "Would" is not used in the if-clause. | had vaccinated :: The people are the receivers. | The if-clause uses the past perfect passive.

ec | S | The government has failed to take appropriate measures in order to tackling poverty. | The government has failed to take appropriate measures in order to tackle poverty. | After "in order to" use the base form. | Infinitives of purpose.
ec | E | He was so angry that he could hardly not speak. | He was so angry that he could hardly speak. | "Hardly not" is a double negative. | Double negatives.
ec | T | The tour starts at nine and last about three hours. | The tour starts at nine and lasts about three hours. | A third-person singular verb needs "-s". | Agreement.
