# A2 modals, prepositions, articles: vc, wb, es, pw, vf
vc | MOD | She ___ (can / swim) when she was four. | could swim | can swim :: The sentence is in the past. | was able swim :: "Was able" needs "to". | can swam :: "Can" is followed by the base form. | "Could swim" describes ability in the past.
vc | MOD | I ___ (must / go) to the dentist tomorrow. | must go | must to go :: "Must" is not followed by "to". | musts go :: "Must" never takes -s. | am must go :: "Am must" is not grammatical. | "Must go" shows obligation.
vc | MOD | They ___ (might / be) late because of the traffic. | might be | might to be :: "Might" is not followed by "to". | mights be :: "Might" never takes -s. | may to be :: "May" is not followed by "to". | "Might be" shows possibility.
vc | MOD | ___ I (may / open) the window? | May I open | May I to open :: "May" is not followed by "to". | Do I may open :: "May" never takes "do". | Mays I open :: "May" never takes -s. | "May I open" asks for permission.
vc | MOD | You ___ (should / see) a doctor about that cough. | should see | should to see :: "Should" is not followed by "to". | shoulds see :: "Should" never takes -s. | should saw :: "Should" is followed by the base form. | "Should see" gives advice.
vc | MOD | He ___ (can't / drive) because he is only fifteen. | can't drive | doesn't can drive :: "Can" never takes "doesn't". | can't to drive :: "Can't" is not followed by "to". | cans drive :: "Can" never takes -s. | "Can't drive" shows inability.
vc | MOD | We ___ (have to / wear) a uniform at school. | have to wear | has to wear :: "We" takes "have to". | must to wear :: "Must" is not followed by "to". | have wear :: "Have" needs "to" here. | "Have to wear" shows a rule.
vc | MOD | She ___ (have to / work) on Saturdays. | has to work | have to work :: "She" takes "has to". | haves to work :: "Have" does not become "haves". | musts work :: "Must" never takes -s. | "Has to work" is the third person form.
vc | MOD | ___ you (can / help) me, please? | Can you help | Do you can help :: "Can" never takes "do". | Can you to help :: "Can" is not followed by "to". | You can help :: A question needs the modal first. | "Can you help" is a request.

vc | MOD | I ___ (couldn't / sleep) because it was too hot. | couldn't sleep | can't sleep :: The sentence is in the past. | didn't could sleep :: "Could" does not use "didn't". | couldn't slept :: "Could" is followed by the base form. | "Couldn't sleep" describes past inability.
vc | MOD | She ___ (must / be) very tired after the trip. | must be | must to be :: "Must" is not followed by "to". | musts be :: "Must" never takes -s. | has to be :: "Has to be" shows obligation. | "Must be" is a logical conclusion.
vf | MOD | They ___ park their car on this street. It's allowed. | can | mustn't :: "Mustn't" means forbidden. | needn't :: "Needn't" means it is not necessary. | shouldn't :: "Shouldn't" is advice against. | "Can" shows permission.
vf | MOD | ___ you like to come to dinner on Friday? | Would | Do :: "Do you like to come" is a question about habit. | Can :: "Can you like" is not an invitation. | Must :: "Must you like" is not an invitation. | "Would you like" is a polite invitation.
vf | MOD | I'm not sure, but it ___ be true. | might | will to :: "Will" is not followed by "to". | has :: "Has be" is not correct. | needs :: "Needs be" is not correct. | "Might be" shows uncertainty.
vf | MOD | You ___ tell lies. It's wrong. | shouldn't | needn't :: "Needn't" means it is not necessary. | might not :: "Might not" means perhaps not. | can :: "Can" would give permission. | "Shouldn't" gives advice not to do something.
vf | MOD | We have an exam tomorrow, so we ___ study tonight. | have to | might to :: "Might" is not followed by "to". | can :: "Can" does not show obligation. | would :: "Would" does not show obligation. | "Have to" shows obligation.
