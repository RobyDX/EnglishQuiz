# C1 pronouns, relative clauses and substitution
mc | PRON | The proposal, ___ details were kept secret until the last moment, was approved without debate. | whose | of which :: "Of which details" would need a different order. | which :: "Which details" does not show possession here. | whom :: "Whom details" is ungrammatical. | "Whose" can show possession for things as well as people.
mc | PRON | The scientists, several of ___ had worked on the project for decades, were reluctant to abandon it. | whom | who :: After "of" the object form is needed. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | them :: "Them" cannot introduce a relative clause. | "Several of whom" is a standard relative construction.
mc | PRON | The report identifies three factors, the most significant of ___ is the lack of investment. | which | whom :: "Whom" refers to people. | that :: "That" cannot follow a preposition. | them :: "Them" cannot introduce a relative clause. | "The most significant of which" introduces a nonrestrictive clause.
mc | PRON | She could not remember ___ she had left the documents, which made her increasingly anxious. | where | which :: "Which" needs an antecedent. | what :: "What" does not fit a question about place. | when :: "When" asks about time. | "Where" introduces an embedded question about place.
mc | PRON | It was the director ___ insisted on a full investigation, not the board. | who | whom :: "Whom" is an object form. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | "Who" is the subject in a cleft sentence.
mc | PRON | What I find most irritating is ___ he never admits he is wrong. | that | what :: "What" would repeat the cleft word. | which :: "Which" needs an antecedent. | how :: "How" changes the meaning. | "That" introduces the noun clause after "is".
mc | PRON | The old and the young alike ___ benefited from the reforms. | have | has :: "The old and the young" is plural. | having :: A finite verb is needed. | had been :: The present perfect fits the sense. | "The old and the young" takes a plural verb.
mc | PRON | They blamed ___ for the failure of the negotiations, each accusing the other of bad faith. | each other | themselves :: "Themselves" would mean each blamed himself. | one :: "One" does not fit here. | others :: "Others" is not a reciprocal pronoun. | "Each other" expresses a mutual action.
mc | PRON | There is little ___ we can do to change the situation now. | that | what :: "What" cannot follow "little" here. | which :: "Which" is awkward after "little". | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | "That" is the usual relative pronoun after "little".
mc | PRON | He has two sons, neither of ___ shows any interest in the family business. | whom | which :: "Which" refers to things. | who :: After "of" the object form is needed. | them :: "Them" cannot introduce a relative clause. | "Neither of whom" is a standard relative construction.
mc | PRON | The reason ___ she resigned was never made public. | why | for which why :: "Why" and "for which" duplicate each other. | what :: "What" cannot follow "reason". | that why :: "That why" is ungrammatical. | "The reason why" is a standard structure.
mc | PRON | The moment ___ the news broke, the shares plummeted. | that | which :: "Which" would need a preposition. | when that :: "When that" is redundant. | what :: "What" cannot follow a noun. | "The moment that" is a standard time conjunction.
fb | PRON | The firm, ___ founder died last year, is now run by his daughters. | whose | of whom :: "Of whom founder" is ungrammatical. | which :: "Which founder" does not show possession. | who :: "Who founder" is ungrammatical. | "Whose" shows possession.
fb | PRON | She is a person on ___ you can always rely in a crisis. | whom | who :: After a preposition use "whom". | which :: "Which" refers to things. | that :: "That" cannot follow a preposition. | "On whom" is the formal pattern.
fb | PRON | The delegates, most of ___ had travelled overnight, looked exhausted. | whom | which :: "Which" refers to things. | them :: "Them" cannot introduce a relative clause. | who :: After "of" the object form is needed. | "Most of whom" is a standard relative construction.
fb | PRON | The company launched a new product, ___ proved to be a huge success. | which | what :: "What" cannot introduce a relative clause. | that :: "That" cannot follow a comma. | who :: "Who" refers to people. | "Which" introduces a nonrestrictive clause.
fb | PRON | ___ the committee decides, the staff will be informed immediately. | Whatever | However :: "However the committee decides" changes the meaning. | Whichever :: "Whichever" needs a limited choice. | Whoever :: "Whoever" refers to people. | "Whatever" means no matter what.
fb | PRON | The two sides have agreed to meet ___ other halfway. | each | one :: "One other" is not the idiom. | the :: "The other halfway" is incomplete. | every :: "Every other" has a different meaning. | "Each other" is the reciprocal pronoun.
fb | PRON | Those ___ wish to attend should register by Friday. | who | whom :: "Whom" is an object form. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | "Those who" refers to people in general.
fb | PRON | ___ is needed is a fresh approach to the problem. | What | That :: "That is needed" lacks a head noun. | Which :: "Which is needed" lacks an antecedent. | Who :: "Who" refers to people. | "What" means "the thing that" in a cleft sentence.
fb | PRON | The chairman, together with his colleagues, ___ expected to attend. | is | are :: The subject "chairman" is singular. | were :: The sentence is in the present. | being :: A finite verb is needed. | "Together with" does not change the number of the subject.
fb | PRON | One should always do ___ best to meet a deadline. | one's | his own :: "His own" excludes the rest of the group. | their :: "Their" is common informally but breaks the formal pattern. | its :: "Its" refers to things. | "One" is followed by "one's" in formal usage.
vf | PRON | Anyone ___ wishes to withdraw must notify the office in writing. | who | whom :: "Whom" is an object form. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | "Who" is the subject.
vf | PRON | I would appreciate ___ if you could keep this confidential. | it | that :: "I would appreciate that if" is awkward. | this :: "This" is less natural. | them :: "Them" has no reference. | "I would appreciate it if" is a fixed polite structure.
vf | PRON | It was not until later ___ she realised the full extent of the damage. | that | when :: "It was not until later when" is informal. | which :: "Which" needs an antecedent. | what :: "What" is ungrammatical here. | "It was not until ... that" is a cleft structure.
vf | PRON | There is nothing ___ pleases her more than a well-organised event. | that | what :: "What" cannot follow "nothing". | which :: "Which" is less usual after "nothing". | who :: "Who" refers to people. | "That" is the usual relative after "nothing".
vc | PRON | Neither of the answers ___ (be) entirely satisfactory. | was | were :: In careful usage "neither" takes a singular verb. | are :: "Neither" takes a singular verb. | being :: A finite verb is needed. | "Neither of" takes a singular verb in formal English.
vc | PRON | He wished to be left to ___ (he) for a while. | himself | him :: "Him" is not reflexive. | his own :: "His own" needs a noun. | he :: "He" is a subject pronoun. | "Left to himself" is a fixed expression.
