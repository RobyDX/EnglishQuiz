# C2 batch b3: grammar categories (articles, pronouns, adjectives, modals, tenses, prepositions)
mc | ART | The council has been criticised for its handling of ___ issue of homelessness. | the | an :: "Issue" begins with a vowel sound but the issue is specific. | no article :: A known issue takes "the". | "The issue" refers to a known topic.
mc | ART | It was a case of ___ blind leading the blind. | the | a :: "A blind" changes the idiom. | no article :: The idiom needs the article. | "The blind leading the blind" is the fixed idiom.
mc | ART | She was ___ first woman to be appointed to the Supreme Court. | the | a :: "A first" is not used for a unique position. | no article :: An ordinal takes "the". | "The first" takes the definite article.
mc | ART | There is ___ difference between being firm and being inflexible. | a | the :: "The difference" would refer to a known one. | no article :: A singular noun needs a determiner. | "A difference" introduces a general distinction.
mc | ART | We were all struck by ___ extent of the damage. | the | an :: "Extent" begins with a vowel sound but is specific. | no article :: A specific noun takes "the". | "The extent of" is the standard phrase.
mc | ART | I'd like to say a few words on ___ behalf of my colleagues. | their | the :: "On the behalf of" is not standard. | a :: "On a behalf of" is ungrammatical. | "On their behalf" is the standard phrase.
mc | ART | He spoke at ___ length about his plans for the company. | considerable | considerate :: "Considerate" means thoughtful. | considering :: "Considering" is a participle. | "Considerable length" means a long time.
mc | ART | ___ number of complaints has risen sharply in recent weeks. | The | A :: "A number of" takes a plural verb. | No article :: A determiner is needed. | "The number of" takes a singular verb.
mc | ADJ | The politician's ___ rhetoric won applause but little real support. | bombastic | bombed :: "Bombed" is a participle. | bomber :: A "bomber" is an aircraft or person. | "Bombastic" means high-sounding but empty.
mc | ADJ | The sculptor's early works are ___ to the point of being unrecognisable as hers. | uncharacteristic | unchanging :: "Unchanging" does not fit. | uncharitable :: "Uncharitable" means unkind. | "Uncharacteristic" means not typical.
mc | ADJ | He gave a ___ account of his own part in the affair, omitting several awkward facts. | selective | select :: "Select" means exclusive. | selected :: "Selected" is a participle. | "Selective" means choosing carefully.
mc | ADJ | The author's style is ___ and often difficult to follow. | convoluted | convolution :: "Convolution" is a noun. | convened :: "Convened" means assembled. | "Convoluted" means complicated.
mc | ADJ | A ___ crowd had gathered outside the courthouse to await the verdict. | restive | restful :: "Restful" means soothing. | restless :: "Restless" is possible but milder. | "Restive" means uneasy and impatient.
mc | ADJ | The decision proved ___ to the company's long-term prospects. | pivotal | pivoted :: "Pivoted" is a participle. | pivot :: "Pivot" is a noun or verb. | "Pivotal" means crucially important.
mc | PRON | The suspects, none of ___ had a previous record, were released on bail. | whom | which :: "Which" refers to things. | them :: "Them" cannot introduce a relative clause. | "None of whom" is a standard relative construction.
mc | PRON | I cannot think of ___ else who would be as suitable. | anyone | someone :: "Someone" is not used after a negative sense. | everyone :: "Everyone" changes the meaning. | "Anyone else" fits after "cannot think of".
mc | PRON | It was only years later ___ the truth emerged. | that | when :: "Only years later when" is unnatural. | which :: "Which" cannot introduce a cleft clause. | "It was ... that" is a cleft structure.
mc | PRON | The committee, ___ members were appointed by the minister, has no real independence. | whose | which :: "Which members" does not show possession. | who :: "Who members" is ungrammatical. | "Whose" shows possession.
mc | PRON | Most of the delegates, several of ___ had travelled overnight, looked exhausted. | whom | who :: After "of" the object form is needed. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | "Several of whom" is a standard relative construction.
mc | MOD | They ___ to have been told about the changes before the announcement. | ought | should to :: "Should" is not followed by "to". | must to :: "Must" is not followed by "to". | "Ought to have been told" expresses an expectation.
mc | MOD | She ___ not have been more courteous; I was most impressed. | could | would to :: "Would to" is ungrammatical. | can :: "Can not have" is not used. | "Could not have been more" is an emphatic idiom.
mc | MOD | I think the matter ___ to be handled with great care. | needs | need to :: "Matter" takes a singular verb. | needing :: A finite verb is needed. | "Needs to be handled" is a passive need.
mc | MOD | We ___ have gone by train, but we preferred to drive. | could | would to :: "Would to" is ungrammatical. | must :: "Must have gone" expresses deduction. | "Could have" expresses an unused possibility.
mc | VT | The more I read about the case, the less I ___ what the court decided. | understand | am understanding :: "Understand" is a stative verb. | have understood :: The correlative uses the same tense. | The correlative uses the present simple.
mc | VT | By the time you receive this letter, I ___ for Australia. | will have left | will leave :: The future perfect shows completion. | have left :: The present perfect does not fit a future point. | The future perfect shows completion before a future point.
mc | VT | The committee ___ for three hours when the chairman finally called for a vote. | had been meeting | has been meeting :: The present perfect does not fit. | met :: The simple past does not show duration. | The past perfect continuous shows duration before an event.
mc | VT | He has become a different man ___ he lost his job. | since | for :: "For" needs a length of time. | from :: "From" does not link to now. | "Since" marks the starting point.
mc | PREP | The firm has been fined ___ breaching safety regulations. | for | of :: "Fined of" is not standard. | by :: "Fined by" introduces an agent. | "Fined for" introduces the offence.
mc | PREP | His speech was a tribute ___ the courage of ordinary people. | to | for :: "Tribute for" is less standard. | of :: "Tribute of" has another sense. | "A tribute to" is the standard collocation.
mc | PREP | There is an urgent need ___ reform of the tax system. | for | of :: "Need of" is possible but "need for" is standard. | to :: "Need to" is followed by a verb. | "Need for" is the standard collocation.
mc | PREP | She was greatly influenced ___ the writings of the early feminists. | by | from :: "Influenced from" is not standard. | of :: "Influenced of" is not standard. | "Influenced by" introduces the source.
fb | ART | I had ___ idea what she was talking about. | no | not :: "Not idea" is ungrammatical. | none :: "None idea" is ungrammatical. | "No idea" is a negative determiner phrase.
fb | ART | The matter will be decided by ___ vote of the whole membership. | a | the :: "The vote" would refer to a known vote. | an :: "Vote" begins with a consonant sound. | "A vote of" introduces a new item.
fb | ART | There are ___ many exceptions to the rule that it is hardly a rule at all. | so | such :: "Such many" is ungrammatical. | very :: "Very ... that" does not form a result here. | "So many ... that" shows a result.
fb | ART | Hardly ___ anyone remembers the original proposal now. | no article | the :: "Hardly the anyone" is ungrammatical. | an :: "Hardly an anyone" is ungrammatical. | "Hardly anyone" needs no article.
fb | ADJ | Her speech was ___ and delivered without notes. | impromptu | impeccable :: "Impeccable" means faultless. | impassioned :: "Impassioned" means full of feeling. | "Impromptu" means unprepared.
fb | ADJ | The results were ___ , neither confirming nor refuting the theory. | equivocal | equitable :: "Equitable" means fair. | equable :: "Equable" means steady in temper. | "Equivocal" means open to several interpretations.
fb | ADJ | The ministers gave a ___ denial, refusing to answer any further questions. | curt | curb :: A "curb" is a restraint. | curd :: "Curd" is a dairy product. | "Curt" means abruptly brief.
fb | PRON | I would be grateful for any help ___ you can give. | that | what :: "What" cannot follow a noun. | whom :: "Whom" refers to people. | "That" introduces a defining relative clause.
fb | PRON | The staff, ___ of them were new, struggled to cope with the rush. | most | mostly :: "Mostly" is an adverb. | almost :: "Almost of them" is ungrammatical. | "Most of them" is a standard quantifier.
fb | PRON | The man ___ lives next door is a retired judge. | who | whom :: The relative acts as the subject. | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | "Who" is the subject of the relative clause.
fb | MOD | You ___ well wonder why the committee took so long to decide. | may | will :: "Will well" is ungrammatical. | must :: "Must well" is ungrammatical. | "May well wonder" is a fixed phrase.
fb | MOD | He ___ to have been warned, but nobody had the courage to speak. | ought | should to :: "Should" is not followed by "to". | must :: "Must to have" is ungrammatical. | "Ought to have been warned" expresses an expectation.
fb | MOD | It is important that every employee ___ the new regulations before Friday. | read | reads :: The mandative subjunctive uses the base form. | will read :: "Will" is not used here. | "Important that" takes the base form.
fb | MOD | They ___ not have been happier with the outcome. | could | would to :: "Would to" is ungrammatical. | can :: "Can not have" is not used. | "Could not have been happier" is an emphatic idiom.
fb | VT | Hardly ___ the announcement been made when the protests began. | had | has :: The past perfect is needed. | did :: "Hardly did the announcement been" is ungrammatical. | "Hardly had" is the standard inversion.
fb | VT | She ___ for the company for twelve years when it was taken over. | had been working | has been working :: The present perfect does not fit. | worked :: The simple past does not show duration. | The past perfect continuous shows duration before an event.
fb | PREP | Several students were suspended ___ cheating in the examination. | for | of :: "Suspended of" is not standard. | by :: "Suspended by" introduces an agent. | "Suspended for" introduces the reason.
fb | PREP | The ministry has no intention ___ revising its policy. | of | to :: "Intention to" is followed by a verb. | for :: "Intention for" is not standard. | "Intention of" is followed by -ing.
fb | PREP | A lot depends ___ the outcome of the negotiations. | on | of :: "Depends of" is not standard. | at :: "Depends at" is not standard. | "Depend on" is the standard collocation.
fb | PREP | He was greatly in awe ___ his father. | of | for :: "Awe for" is not standard. | to :: "Awe to" is not standard. | "In awe of" is the fixed phrase.
