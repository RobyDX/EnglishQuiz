# C1 modal verbs and related structures
mc | MOD | The committee ___ have reached its decision hastily, given how little time the members had to consider the evidence. | may well | may as well :: "May as well" suggests that there is nothing better to do, not a probability. | might as well :: "Might as well" also means there is no good alternative. | can well :: "Can well" is not an idiomatic way to express probability. | "May well have" expresses a strong probability about the past.
mc | MOD | You ___ have told me earlier that the deadline had been brought forward; now it is far too late to rearrange anything. | could at least | needn't have :: "Needn't have" would say that telling me was unnecessary, which contradicts the complaint. | mustn't have :: "Mustn't have" is not used for criticism of a past omission. | can't have :: "Can't have" is a negative deduction, not a reproach. | "Could at least have" voices a mild reproach about a missed chance.
mc | MOD | Although the plan looked flawless on paper, it ___ never have worked in practice. | could | should :: "Should never have worked" would mean it was wrong that it worked. | ought :: "Ought never have" lacks the infinitive marker in this structure. | might :: "Might never have worked" would leave the possibility open. | "Could never have worked" says it was impossible for it to work.
mc | MOD | Visitors ___ not leave the building without signing out at the reception desk. | are to | ought :: "Ought not leave" lacks "to" and is not a regulation. | may as well :: "May as well not" suggests there is nothing to lose. | would rather :: "Would rather not" expresses a preference, not a rule. | "Are not to" is the formal way of stating a prohibition.
mc | MOD | If you ___ be so kind as to wait a moment, the director will see you shortly. | would | will to :: "Will to" is not a grammatical combination. | should :: "Should be so kind" does not make a polite request. | might :: "Might be so kind" reads as speculation, not a request. | "Would be so kind as to" is a formal polite request.
mc | MOD | She is said to ___ the most gifted pianist of her generation, although she never sought fame. | have been | being :: After "said to" an infinitive is needed. | be being :: A continuous passive form changes the meaning. | had been :: A finite perfect form is not used after "to". | "To have been" refers to an earlier state in reported opinions.
mc | MOD | We had hoped the negotiations would be brief; as it turned out, they ___ go on for months. | were to | were :: Without "to" the verb does not express the unexpected future in the past. | would to :: "Would to" is not grammatical here. | had to be :: "Had to be" means it was necessary, not that fate decreed it. | "Were to" expresses a future event seen from a past viewpoint.
mc | MOD | No sooner had we sat down to dinner ___ the telephone started ringing. | than | when :: "No sooner" is followed by "than". | that :: "That" does not complete the comparison. | as :: "As" does not follow "no sooner". | "No sooner ... than" is a fixed correlative pair.
mc | MOD | I wasn't expecting the proposal, but I ___ say I find it quite appealing. | must | should :: "Should say" gives advice, not an admission. | ought :: "Ought say" lacks "to". | need :: "Need say" does not express an admission. | "I must say" is a fixed way of adding emphasis to an opinion.
mc | MOD | It's high time the government ___ serious steps to curb inflation. | took | takes :: After "it's high time" a past form is required. | would take :: "Would take" is not used after "it's high time". | has taken :: The present perfect does not fit the structure. | "It's high time" + past simple states that action is overdue.
mc | MOD | Whatever the outcome, we ___ be grateful for the opportunity to take part. | shall | should :: "Should" would suggest obligation rather than determination. | might :: "Might" would weaken the commitment. | can :: "Can" does not express firm intention. | "Shall" in a first-person statement expresses firm intention.
mc | MOD | He ___ to have left the building by now; his car is no longer in the car park. | ought | should to :: "Should" is not followed by "to". | must :: "Must to have" is ungrammatical. | would :: "Would to have" is ungrammatical. | "Ought to have" expresses a reasonable expectation or deduction.
mc | MOD | Much as I'd ___ to help, I'm afraid my schedule makes it impossible. | like | liking :: After "I'd" use the base form. | liked :: "liked" does not work here, because after "I'd" use the base form. | likely :: "Likely" is an adjective. | "Much as I'd like to" is a concessive structure.
mc | MOD | There's no knowing how the markets ___ react to the announcement. | will | to :: After "how" a finite verb is needed. | would to :: "Would to" is ungrammatical. | shall to :: "Shall to" is ungrammatical. | "There's no knowing" is followed by a wh-clause.
mc | MOD | They ___ well have anticipated the reaction, but they said nothing at the time. | may | would :: "Would well have" does not express probability. | must :: "Must well have" is not idiomatic. | shall :: "Shall well have" is not idiomatic. | "May well have" expresses a strong possibility.
fb | MOD | Try as he ___, he could not persuade the board to change its mind. | might | may :: In a concessive past context "might" is the usual form. | would :: "Would" does not fit the "try as he might" idiom. | could :: "Try as he could" is rarely used. | "Try as he might" means however hard he tried.
fb | MOD | You ___ well have saved yourself the trouble by checking the instructions first. | might as | may as :: "May as well" can be used but "might as well" suits the tone. | could as :: "Could as well" is not the fixed phrase. | would as :: "Would as well" is not the fixed phrase. | "Might as well have" means it would have been equally easy.
fb | MOD | The new regulations ___ not be interpreted as a licence to ignore safety standards. | are to | ought :: "Ought not be" lacks "to". | had better :: "Had better not be" is informal advice. | would :: "Would not be interpreted" changes the meaning. | "Are not to be" is a formal prohibition.
fb | MOD | Had she been a little more careful, the accident ___ never have happened. | would | will :: "Will have" does not fit an unreal past. | should to :: "Should to" is ungrammatical. | must :: "Must never have happened" is a deduction. | "Would never have happened" completes the third conditional.
fb | MOD | I'd sooner ___ than apologise to him after what he said. | resign | resigning :: "Would sooner" is followed by the base form. | to resign :: "to resign" does not work here, because "Would sooner" is followed by the base form. | resigned :: "resigned" does not work here, because "Would sooner" is followed by the base form. | "Would sooner ... than" is followed by the base form.
fb | MOD | The medicine ___ not be taken with alcohol under any circumstances. | is to | would :: "Would not be taken" does not give an instruction. | may :: "May not be taken" is a weaker permission statement. | has :: "Has not be taken" is ungrammatical. | "Is not to be taken" is a formal instruction.
fb | MOD | He ___ not have been more than twenty when he founded the company. | can't | mustn't :: "Mustn't have" is not used for deductions. | needn't :: "Needn't have" says something was unnecessary. | shan't :: "Shan't" expresses refusal. | "Can't have been" is a negative deduction.
fb | MOD | In the unlikely event that the flight ___ be cancelled, passengers will be offered a full refund. | should | would :: "Would" does not form the formal conditional. | shall :: "Shall" is not used in this structure. | might to :: "Might to" is ungrammatical. | "Should" in an if-clause signals a remote possibility.
vf | MOD | The proposal strikes me as sensible, and I ___ add that it is also remarkably inexpensive. | might | will to :: "Will" is not followed by "to" here. | have :: "Have add" is ungrammatical. | being :: A participle does not fit. | "Might add" introduces a further point tentatively.
vf | MOD | She ___ to be told twice; she always grasps instructions at once. | never needs | never need :: With "never" as the main negative the verb takes "-s". | doesn't needs :: "Doesn't" is followed by the base form. | never needing :: A finite verb is needed. | "Never needs to" is a regular present form.
vf | MOD | It ___ be pointed out that the figures were based on provisional data. | should | had better :: "Had better be pointed out" is informal. | might as well :: "Might as well" does not fit a formal remark. | would rather :: "Would rather" expresses preference. | "Should be pointed out" is a formal way of drawing attention to a fact.
vf | MOD | We ___ have gone abroad for the holiday, but the cost of flights put us off. | could | had better :: "Had better have gone" is not used. | would rather :: "Would rather have gone" changes the structure. | ought :: "Ought have" lacks "to". | "Could have" expresses a possibility that was not taken up.
vf | MOD | Few people ___ have predicted that the small start-up would overtake its larger rivals. | could | needn't :: "Needn't have predicted" says it was unnecessary. | mustn't :: "Mustn't have" is not used here. | shan't :: "Shan't" is a refusal. | "Could have predicted" refers to a past ability.
vf | MOD | He ___ be reached at the office on weekdays, but never at weekends. | can | would to :: "Would to" is ungrammatical. | ought :: "Ought be reached" lacks "to". | had :: "Had be reached" is ungrammatical. | "Can be reached" states a possibility of contact.
vf | MOD | I ___ rather you didn't mention the matter to anyone for the time being. | would | had :: "Had rather" is dated. | should :: "Should rather" is rare. | might :: "Might rather" is not used. | "I would rather you didn't" is a polite request.
vf | MOD | If I ___ able to choose again, I would take exactly the same decision. | were | would be :: "Would" is not used in the if-clause. | will be :: "Will" is not used in the if-clause. | am :: The second conditional needs a past form. | "If I were able" is an unreal present condition.
vc | MOD | It's essential that every candidate ___ (submit) the form before the end of the month. | submit | submits :: The mandative subjunctive uses the base form. | submitted :: "submitted" does not work here, because the mandative subjunctive uses the base form. | will submit :: "Will" is not used after "it's essential that". | The mandative subjunctive after "essential" takes the base form.
vc | MOD | I demand that he ___ (apologise) to everyone who was affected. | apologise | apologises :: The mandative subjunctive uses the base form. | apologised :: "apologised" does not work here, because the mandative subjunctive uses the base form. | would apologise :: "Would" is not used after "demand that". | "Demand that" takes the base form.
vc | MOD | The doctor insisted that she ___ (rest) for at least a fortnight. | rest | rests :: The mandative subjunctive uses the base form. | rested :: "rested" does not work here, because the mandative subjunctive uses the base form. | would rest :: "Would" is not used after "insisted that". | "Insist that" takes the base form.
vc | MOD | If it ___ (be) not for her timely intervention, the project would have collapsed. | had not been | was not :: The third conditional needs the past perfect. | were not :: This refers to the present. | would not be :: "Would" is not used in the if-clause. | "If it had not been for" means "without".
vc | MOD | The report suggests that the minister ___ (resign) before the investigation begins. | resign | resigns :: The mandative subjunctive uses the base form. | resigned :: "resigned" does not work here, because the mandative subjunctive uses the base form. | will resign :: "Will" does not follow "suggests that" in the mandative. | "Suggest that" can take the base form.
vc | MOD | Were the company ___ (offer) a better salary, I might reconsider my decision. | to offer | offering :: After "were the company" an infinitive is needed. | offer :: "Were ... to" requires the infinitive with "to". | offered :: A participle does not fit the inversion. | "Were + subject + to + infinitive" is a formal conditional.
vc | MOD | I would rather he ___ (not / know) the details of the arrangement. | didn't know | doesn't know :: After "would rather" + subject a past form is used. | wouldn't know :: "Would" is not used here. | hasn't known :: The present perfect does not fit. | A past form expresses a present preference.
es | MOD | You [mustn't] have worried about the result; I was confident from the start. | needn't | "Needn't have" says the worry was unnecessary.
es | MOD | It is vital that he [attends] the meeting in person. | attend | The mandative subjunctive takes the base form.
es | MOD | He [might] has missed the train. | have | After "might" use "have".
es | MOD | If he [would] have known, he would have helped. | had | "Would" is not used in the if-clause.
es | MOD | The management [demanded] that she resigns at once. | resign | After "demand that" the base form is used.
ec | MOD | It is essential that she signs the contract before Friday. | It is essential that she sign the contract before Friday. | After "essential that" the mandative subjunctive takes the base form. | Mandative subjunctive.
ec | MOD | I would rather you won't tell anyone about my plans. | I would rather you didn't tell anyone about my plans. | After "would rather" + subject use a past form. | Would rather.
ec | MOD | Not until he arrived did he realised the mistake. | Not until he arrived did he realise the mistake. | After inverted "did" use the base form. | Inversion.
ec | MOD | They shouldn't have been told us the truth; it only caused panic. | They shouldn't have told us the truth; it only caused panic. | The active perfect infinitive is needed. | Modal perfect.
ec | MOD | She is thought to be left the country last week. | She is thought to have left the country last week. | A perfect infinitive is needed for an earlier event. | Reporting structures.
ec | MOD | You needn't to have apologised; nobody was offended. | You needn't have apologised; nobody was offended. | "Needn't" is followed by "have", not "to have". | Modal perfect.
tr | MOD | I am sure she did not mean to offend you. | CAN'T | She ___ to offend you. | can't have meant | "Can't have" expresses a negative deduction.
tr | MOD | It is a pity you did not tell me earlier. | WISH | I ___ me earlier. | wish you had told | "Wish" + past perfect expresses regret.
tr | MOD | It is time the government acted on this matter. | HIGH | It is ___ on this matter. | high time the government acted | "High time" + past simple states that action is overdue.
tr | MOD | The workers did not have to do overtime. | NEED | The workers ___ overtime. | needn't have done ;; didn't need to do | "Needn't have" or "didn't need to" expresses no necessity.
tr | MOD | He was to inherit the estate, but he died first. | NEVER | He ___ the estate. | never lived to inherit | An idiom expressing an unrealised future in the past.
tr | MOD | I strongly suggest that you reconsider. | RECONSIDER | I strongly recommend that you ___. | reconsider | The mandative subjunctive takes the base form.
tr | MOD | There is no chance that they will agree. | BOUND | They ___ disagree. | are bound to | "Be bound to" expresses near-certainty.
tr | MOD | Under no circumstances should staff open this door. | MUST | Staff ___ this door under any circumstances. | must not open | "Must not" expresses prohibition.
