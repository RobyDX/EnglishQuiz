# C2 batch a1: mc and fb, advanced vocabulary and grammar
mc | W | The minister's resignation was ___ by a series of leaks that undermined confidence in her department. | precipitated | precluded :: "Precluded" means prevented. | procrastinated :: "Procrastinated" means delayed. | prescribed :: "Prescribed" means ordered. | "Precipitated" means brought about suddenly.
mc | W | The auditors found that the accounts had been ___ to conceal the extent of the losses. | falsified | fortified :: "Fortified" means strengthened. | fabricated :: "Fabricated" means invented from nothing. | classified :: "Classified" means categorised or made secret. | "Falsified" means altered to deceive.
mc | W | Negotiators have been working ___ to bridge the gulf between the two delegations. | assiduously | ambiguously :: "Ambiguously" means unclearly. | arbitrarily :: "Arbitrarily" means without reason. | audaciously :: "Audaciously" means boldly. | "Assiduously" means with constant care and effort.
mc | W | Her ___ attention to detail ensured that no error survived the final proofreading. | meticulous | mischievous :: "Mischievous" means playfully naughty. | momentous :: "Momentous" means very important. | mundane :: "Mundane" means ordinary. | "Meticulous" means extremely careful.
mc | S | The findings are ___ with the hypothesis that the climate shifted abruptly during this period. | consonant | consecutive :: "Consecutive" means following in sequence. | consequent :: "Consequent" means resulting. | consolatory :: "Consolatory" means comforting. | "Consonant with" means in agreement with.
mc | S | Only a ___ of the samples showed any trace of the contaminant. | fraction | faction :: A "faction" is a small group within a larger one. | friction :: "Friction" is resistance to movement. | fiction :: "Fiction" is invented writing. | A "fraction" is a small part.
mc | S | The theory, though elegant, has so far proved ___ to experimental verification. | impervious | impetuous :: "Impetuous" means rash. | impudent :: "Impudent" means rude. | impartial :: "Impartial" means fair. | "Impervious to" means not affected by.
mc | S | Epidemiologists warn that the virus could ___ unnoticed in remote communities for months. | proliferate | prolong :: "Prolong" means to extend. | prohibit :: "Prohibit" means to forbid. | proclaim :: "Proclaim" means to announce. | "Proliferate" means to multiply rapidly.
mc | E | His ___ remarks about her appearance left her feeling thoroughly humiliated. | disparaging | dispassionate :: "Dispassionate" means unemotional. | disparate :: "Disparate" means fundamentally different. | disposable :: "Disposable" means to be thrown away. | "Disparaging" means belittling.
mc | E | The two brothers had been ___ for years, communicating only through their solicitors. | estranged | endangered :: "Endangered" means at risk. | entangled :: "Entangled" means caught up. | enraged :: "Enraged" means very angry. | "Estranged" means no longer on friendly terms.
mc | E | She greeted the news with ___ calm, as if nothing could possibly disturb her. | studied | stunted :: "Stunted" means stopped from growing. | sturdy :: "Sturdy" means strongly built. | stilted :: "Stilted" means stiff and unnatural in speech. | "Studied" means deliberately cultivated.
mc | E | After the scandal, he became something of a ___ in the village, avoided by his former friends. | pariah | paragon :: A "paragon" is a model of excellence. | parasite :: A "parasite" lives off another. | paradox :: A "paradox" is a contradiction. | A "pariah" is an outcast.
mc | T | The remote lodge, ___ in the folds of the hills, can be reached only on foot. | nestled | nuzzled :: "Nuzzled" means rubbed gently with the nose. | nurtured :: "Nurtured" means cared for. | notched :: "Notched" means cut with small marks. | "Nestled" means sheltered snugly.
mc | T | The town's ___ charm has survived despite the flood of visitors each summer. | unassuming | unsparing :: "Unsparing" means merciless. | unwitting :: "Unwitting" means unaware. | unyielding :: "Unyielding" means firm. | "Unassuming" means modest.
mc | T | The cruise ship ___ the coast for three days before docking at its final port. | skirted | skimmed :: "Skimmed" means passed lightly over a surface. | skewered :: "Skewered" means pierced. | scuttled :: "Scuttled" means deliberately sank. | "Skirted" means passed along the edge of.
mc | T | They set off at dawn, ___ to cover the whole ridge before the weather turned. | resolved | resolute :: "Resolute" is an adjective and needs "to be". | resolving :: "Resolving" does not fit the participle clause. | resolution :: "Resolution" is a noun. | "Resolved to" means determined to.
mc | MOD | Not for a moment ___ that the plan might fail. | did she suspect | she suspected :: A negative adverbial at the start needs inversion. | she did suspect :: The inversion is missing. | suspected she :: The auxiliary is missing. | "Not for a moment" requires inversion with "did".
mc | MOD | Were it not for the generosity of its donors, the museum ___ closed years ago. | would have | will have :: "Will" does not fit an unreal past. | had :: A modal is needed in the result clause. | would :: "Would closed" lacks "have". | The result clause of a third conditional uses "would have" + participle.
mc | MOD | However much he ___ to deny it, the evidence pointed squarely at him. | might wish | will wish :: "However much" takes "might" or the present for concession. | wished to :: The sentence is incomplete. | has wished :: The perfect does not express concession here. | "However much he might wish" expresses concession.
mc | VT | No sooner ___ the regulations been relaxed than companies began to take advantage of them. | had | have :: "No sooner" takes the past perfect. | did :: "Did the regulations been" is ungrammatical. | were :: "Were the regulations been" is ungrammatical. | "No sooner had" is the standard inversion.
mc | VT | I was under the impression that you ___ the contract before you left the office. | had signed | have signed :: The present perfect does not fit a past reference. | would sign :: This refers to the future in the past. | were signing :: The continuous does not show completion. | The past perfect shows an earlier completed action.
mc | ART | It is only in ___ the last few decades that the scale of the problem has been recognised. | no article | the :: "In the last few decades" already has "the". | a :: The phrase "in last" is ungrammatical. | those :: "In those the last" is ungrammatical. | The phrase "the last few decades" needs no extra article.
mc | ART | She spoke with ___ conviction that nobody in the hall doubted her sincerity. | such | so :: "So conviction" is ungrammatical. | such a :: "Conviction" is uncountable. | very :: "Very conviction" is ungrammatical. | "Such" precedes an uncountable noun in a result clause.
mc | ART | There is ___ to be said for waiting until the market stabilises. | much | many :: "Much" is used with uncountable pronouns. | a few :: "A few to be said" is ungrammatical. | few :: "Few to be said" is ungrammatical. | "Much to be said for" is the fixed phrase.
mc | PRON | The committee, ___ of whom had expected such a ruling, met in emergency session. | few | little :: "Little of whom" is ungrammatical. | less :: "Less of whom" is ungrammatical. | fewer :: "Fewer of whom" implies a comparison. | "Few of whom" is a standard relative construction.
mc | PRON | The report highlights the extent ___ the government has failed to honour its pledges. | to which | in which :: "In which" does not fit "extent". | of which :: "Of which" does not fit "extent". | at which :: "At which" does not fit "extent". | "The extent to which" is the standard phrase.
mc | PRON | It was the manager herself ___ was held accountable for the failure. | who | whom :: The relative acts as the subject. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | "Who" is the subject of the cleft clause.
mc | PREP | The two parties have been at loggerheads ___ the allocation of funds. | over | at :: "At loggerheads at" is not standard. | to :: "At loggerheads to" is not standard. | by :: "At loggerheads by" is not standard. | "Over" introduces the matter of dispute.
mc | PREP | The decision was reached ___ consultation with representatives of all the member states. | in | on :: "On consultation" is not standard. | at :: "At consultation" is not standard. | by :: "By consultation" is not standard. | "In consultation with" is the fixed phrase.
mc | ADJ | The performance was ___ short of the standard we had come to expect from the company. | far | very :: "Very short of" is unnatural. | much :: "Much short of" is unusual. | so :: "So short of" needs a result clause. | "Far short of" means well below.
mc | ADJ | She is by no means ___ to criticism, as her sharp reaction showed. | impervious | previous :: "Previous" means earlier. | pervious :: This is rare and means permeable. | impious :: "Impious" means irreligious. | "Impervious to" means unaffected by.
mc | CONF | The government's policy was widely ___ as a betrayal of the voters who had supported it. | perceived | conceived :: "Conceived" means formed in the mind. | received :: "Received" means accepted or got. | deceived :: "Deceived" means misled. | "Perceived as" means regarded as.
mc | CONF | The defendant was ___ of all the charges after a trial lasting six weeks. | acquitted | acquired :: "Acquired" means obtained. | acquainted :: "Acquainted" means familiar. | accused :: "Accused" means charged. | "Acquitted" means found not guilty.
mc | PASS | The suspect is understood ___ the country shortly before the warrant was issued. | to have left | to leave :: The perfect infinitive refers to an earlier time. | having left :: "Understood" takes an infinitive. | leaving :: A participle does not follow "understood". | The perfect infinitive shows an earlier time.
mc | PASS | There is no question of the proposals ___ approved without amendment. | being | to be :: "Question of" is followed by a gerund. | be :: A gerund is needed after "of". | having :: "Having approved" is active. | A passive gerund follows "of".
mc | COND | Had the board acted sooner, the crisis ___ averted. | might have been | might be :: This refers to the present. | might had been :: "Had been" is wrong after a modal. | may have been :: "May" does not suit an unreal past. | "Might have been" completes the inverted third conditional.
fb | W | The merger, which had looked certain only a week earlier, finally ___ over price. | foundered | founded :: "Founded" means established. | flourished :: "Flourished" means thrived. | fluctuated :: "Fluctuated" means varied. | "Foundered" means failed.
fb | W | The CEO was ___ to resign after the company's accounts were found to contain serious irregularities. | compelled | compiled :: "Compiled" means put together. | complied :: "Complied" means obeyed. | complemented :: "Complemented" means completed. | "Compelled" means forced.
fb | W | The agreement is ___ on the approval of both parliaments. | contingent | contiguous :: "Contiguous" means adjacent. | contemptuous :: "Contemptuous" means scornful. | contrite :: "Contrite" means remorseful. | "Contingent on" means dependent on.
fb | W | The firm's ___ rise to prominence astonished even its keenest supporters. | meteoric | metallic :: "Metallic" means like metal. | metaphoric :: "Metaphoric" means figurative. | methodical :: "Methodical" means orderly. | "Meteoric" means extremely rapid.
fb | S | Scientists are cautious about ___ the results beyond the narrow conditions of the experiment. | extrapolating | extracting :: "Extracting" means pulling out. | extinguishing :: "Extinguishing" means putting out. | exterminating :: "Exterminating" means destroying. | "Extrapolating" means extending beyond known data.
fb | S | The drug has been shown to ___ the symptoms without addressing the underlying cause. | alleviate | alienate :: "Alienate" means to estrange. | allocate :: "Allocate" means to assign. | alternate :: "Alternate" means to take turns. | "Alleviate" means to make less severe.
fb | S | The study's ___ sample size limits the conclusions that can be drawn. | modest | moderate :: "Moderate" is possible but weaker here. | modish :: "Modish" means fashionable. | modular :: "Modular" means made of units. | "Modest" means small in scale.
fb | E | There was a ___ silence in the room after he had finished speaking. | pregnant | prevalent :: "Prevalent" means widespread. | prominent :: "Prominent" means conspicuous. | presumptuous :: "Presumptuous" means overconfident. | A "pregnant silence" is full of meaning.
fb | E | He was too ___ to admit that he had been mistaken, even when the evidence was overwhelming. | stubborn | sturdy :: "Sturdy" means strongly built. | sullen :: "Sullen" means gloomily silent. | subtle :: "Subtle" means delicate. | "Stubborn" means refusing to change one's mind.
fb | E | The speaker's ___ wit kept the audience entertained throughout the lengthy ceremony. | irrepressible | irreproachable :: "Irreproachable" means faultless. | irresolute :: "Irresolute" means undecided. | irrevocable :: "Irrevocable" means unalterable. | "Irrepressible" means impossible to restrain.
fb | T | The narrow lanes of the old quarter are a ___ of tiny shops and cafés. | warren | warden :: A "warden" supervises. | warrant :: A "warrant" is an authorisation. | warrior :: A "warrior" is a fighter. | A "warren" is a maze of passages.
fb | T | The hotel boasts ___ views across the bay to the distant mountains. | sweeping | swerving :: "Swerving" means turning sharply. | sweltering :: "Sweltering" means very hot. | swaying :: "Swaying" means moving from side to side. | "Sweeping" means wide and extensive.
fb | MOD | Try ___ he might, he could not move the boulder. | as | how :: "Try how" is ungrammatical. | though :: "Try though" lacks the inversion. | what :: "Try what" is ungrammatical. | "Try as he might" expresses concession.
fb | MOD | You ___ well imagine my surprise when I discovered that the letter had been opened. | may | will :: "Will well" is ungrammatical. | must :: "Must well" is ungrammatical. | should :: "Should well" has another meaning. | "May well imagine" is a fixed phrase.
fb | MOD | He ___ to have retired years ago, but he cannot bring himself to let go. | ought | should to :: "Should" is not followed by "to". | must :: "Must to have" is ungrammatical. | would :: "Would to have" is ungrammatical. | "Ought to have" expresses an expectation.
fb | VT | Hardly ___ the plane touched down when the passengers began to unfasten their seat belts. | had | has :: The past perfect is needed. | did :: "Hardly did the plane touched" is ungrammatical. | was :: "Hardly was the plane touched" is ungrammatical. | "Hardly had" is the standard inversion.
fb | VT | At the time of writing, the situation ___ rapidly, and the outcome is unpredictable. | is developing | develops :: The present simple does not suit a changing situation. | has developed :: The present perfect shows completion. | developed :: The sentence is in the present. | The present continuous suits a changing situation.
fb | VT | Little ___ she know that the decision would haunt her for the rest of her life. | did | does :: The sentence is in the past. | had :: "Little had she know" is ungrammatical. | was :: "Little was she know" is ungrammatical. | "Little did she know" is the standard inversion.
fb | ART | The decision was taken in ___ face of fierce opposition from within the party. | the | a :: "In a face of" is not idiomatic. | no article :: The phrase needs the article. | his :: "In his face of" is ungrammatical. | "In the face of" is the fixed phrase.
fb | PRON | The author, ___ latest novel has received glowing reviews, is giving a reading tonight. | whose | who :: "Who latest novel" is ungrammatical. | whom :: "Whom latest novel" is ungrammatical. | which :: "Which latest novel" does not show possession. | "Whose" shows possession.
fb | PRON | The two proposals, neither of ___ is entirely satisfactory, will be reviewed next month. | which | whom :: "Whom" refers to people. | them :: "Them" cannot introduce a relative clause. | what :: "What" cannot introduce a relative clause. | "Neither of which" is a standard relative construction.
fb | PRON | It is ___ who must accept responsibility, not the junior staff. | the directors | the directors' :: A possessive is wrong. | directors :: A determiner is needed. | their directors :: "Their" has no antecedent. | The cleft sentence needs a plural noun.
fb | PREP | The company's success is attributable ___ the commitment of its employees. | to | for :: "Attributable for" is not standard. | of :: "Attributable of" is not standard. | by :: "Attributable by" is not standard. | "Attributable to" is the standard collocation.
fb | PREP | She was ___ the impression that the meeting had been cancelled. | under | in :: "In the impression" is not idiomatic. | on :: "On the impression" is not idiomatic. | with :: "With the impression" has another meaning. | "Under the impression" is the fixed phrase.
fb | PREP | The directors were accused ___ negligence in the handling of the funds. | of | for :: "Accused for" is not standard. | with :: "Accused with" is not standard. | by :: "Accused by" introduces an agent. | "Accused of" introduces the alleged offence.
fb | ADJ | The statement was so ___ that it could be interpreted in several different ways. | equivocal | equitable :: "Equitable" means fair. | equable :: "Equable" means even-tempered. | equivalent :: "Equivalent" means equal. | "Equivocal" means open to more than one interpretation.
fb | PASS | The proposal is thought ___ little chance of being approved. | to have | having :: "Thought" takes an infinitive. | has :: A finite verb does not follow "thought". | to has :: "To" takes the base form. | The reporting passive uses "to have".
fb | PASS | Nothing ___ to be gained by prolonging the dispute. | is | are :: "Nothing" takes a singular verb. | were :: The sentence is in the present. | being :: A finite verb is needed. | "Nothing is to be gained" is a passive-like pattern.
fb | COND | I would be only too pleased to help, ___ I able to do so. | were | was :: The inverted conditional uses "were". | am :: The inversion requires "were". | had :: "Had I able" is ungrammatical. | "Were I able" is a formal conditional inversion.
