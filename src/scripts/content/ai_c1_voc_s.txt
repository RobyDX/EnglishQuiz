# C1 vocabulary: society, health, environment
mc | S | The government has come under fire for failing to ___ the root causes of rising inequality. | address | adjourn :: "Adjourn" means to postpone a meeting. | adhere :: "Adhere" means to stick or follow. | adopt :: "Adopt" means to take up. | "Address" a problem means to deal with it.
mc | S | Campaigners have called for a ___ on the sale of single-use plastics. | ban | band :: A "band" is a group or strip. | bar :: "Bar" is a place or a rod. | bane :: "Bane" is a source of trouble. | A "ban" is an official prohibition.
mc | S | The drought has had a devastating ___ on agriculture in the region. | impact | impart :: "Impart" means to give information. | impulse :: An "impulse" is a sudden urge. | impasse :: An "impasse" is a deadlock. | "Impact" means effect.
mc | S | The study found a strong ___ between poor diet and mental illness. | correlation | correspondence :: "Correspondence" means letters or similarity. | correction :: "Correction" means a change to fix an error. | collaboration :: "Collaboration" means working together. | A "correlation" is a connection between two things.
mc | S | Despite the government's assurances, many remain ___ about the safety of the new vaccine. | sceptical | scepticism :: "Scepticism" is a noun. | sceptic :: A "sceptic" is a person. | scepticly :: This word does not exist. | "Sceptical" means doubtful.
mc | S | The new policy is likely to have far-___ consequences for the entire education system. | reaching | ranging :: "Far-ranging" is possible but "far-reaching" is the fixed phrase. | going :: "Far-going" is not standard. | looking :: "Far-looking" has a different meaning. | "Far-reaching" means having wide effects.
mc | S | The patient's condition is ___ and doctors cannot yet predict how he will respond to treatment. | unpredictable | unpredicted :: "Unpredicted" means not foreseen. | unpredictably :: "Unpredictably" is an adverb. | unpredict :: This word does not exist. | "Unpredictable" means impossible to forecast.
mc | S | The region has been ___ by a series of devastating floods in recent years. | ravaged | ravenous :: "Ravenous" means extremely hungry. | ravished :: "Ravished" has a different meaning. | rampaged :: "Rampaged" means behaved wildly. | "Ravaged" means severely damaged.
mc | S | Many elderly people feel ___ from society because they lack access to digital services. | marginalised | marginal :: "Marginal" is an adjective meaning small. | margined :: This word does not exist. | marginalise :: "Marginalise" is the base verb. | "Marginalised" means pushed to the edge of society.
mc | S | The introduction of the sugar tax was widely seen as a ___ step towards tackling obesity. | pioneering | pioneer :: A "pioneer" is a person. | pioneered :: "Pioneered" is a past participle. | pioneers :: "Pioneers" is a plural noun. | "Pioneering" means innovative.
mc | S | The committee's findings ___ serious doubts about the safety of the product. | cast | threw :: "Threw doubts" is not the collocation. | gave :: "Gave doubts" is not the collocation. | put :: "Put doubts" is not the collocation. | "Cast doubt on" is the fixed collocation.
fb | S | The new housing development has been met ___ fierce opposition from local residents. | with | by :: "Met by opposition" is possible but "with" suggests encountering. | in :: "Met in opposition" is not standard. | to :: "Met to opposition" is not standard. | "Meet with opposition" means to encounter it.
fb | S | Experts predict that sea levels will ___ by up to a metre by the end of the century. | rise | raise :: "Raise" needs an object. | arise :: "Arise" means to appear. | arouse :: "Arouse" means to awaken. | "Rise" does not take an object.
fb | S | Smoking is the leading ___ of preventable death in many countries. | cause | course :: A "course" is a route. | clause :: A "clause" is part of a sentence. | cure :: A "cure" ends an illness. | A "cause" produces an effect.
fb | S | The disease ___ rapidly through the overcrowded camp before aid could arrive. | spread | spreaded :: "Spread" is irregular. | spreads :: The sentence is in the past. | spreading :: An auxiliary is missing. | "Spread" is the past of "spread".
fb | S | Campaigners argue that the law does not go far ___ to protect wildlife. | enough | too :: "Too" does not follow "far" like this. | very :: "Very" does not fit here. | much :: "Much" does not complete the phrase. | "Far enough" means sufficiently.
fb | S | The charity has been ___ funds for a new shelter for the homeless. | raising | rising :: "Rising" does not take an object. | arising :: "Arising" does not take an object. | rousing :: "Rousing" means stirring. | "Raise funds" means to collect money.
vf | S | If governments ___ earlier, the worst effects of climate change could have been avoided. | had acted | acted :: The third conditional needs the past perfect. | would act :: "Would" is not used in the if-clause. | act :: A present form does not fit. | The if-clause uses the past perfect.
vf | S | The report, ___ findings were published last week, has prompted calls for reform. | whose | which :: "Which findings" does not show possession. | that :: "That" cannot follow a comma. | of which :: "Of which findings" is awkward. | "Whose" shows possession.
vf | S | Never before ___ so many people been displaced by conflict in the region. | have | has :: "So many people" is plural. | had :: "Never before" with the present perfect needs "have". | did :: "Did so many people been" is ungrammatical. | "Never before" requires inversion.
vf | S | The number of people living in poverty ___ risen sharply over the past decade. | has | have :: "The number of" takes a singular verb. | had :: The present perfect is needed. | is :: "Is risen" is not a correct perfect. | "The number of" takes a singular verb.
vc | S | By 2050, the world's population ___ (reach) almost ten billion. | will have reached | will reach :: The future perfect shows completion before a future time. | reaches :: A present tense does not fit. | has reached :: The present perfect does not fit. | "By 2050" calls for the future perfect.
vc | S | The measures ___ (introduce) last year have had little effect on the crime rate. | introduced | introducing :: A reduced relative clause needs a participle. | were introduced :: The clause is reduced, so "were" is not used. | introduce :: A participle is needed. | A past participle forms a reduced passive relative clause.
es | S | There has been a sharp rise [of] the number of cyclists. | in | "A rise in" is the fixed collocation.
es | S | The charity is dependent [of] voluntary donations. | on | "Dependent on" is the fixed collocation.
ec | S | The government is committed to reduce carbon emissions by 2030. | The government is committed to reducing carbon emissions by 2030. | "Committed to" is followed by -ing. | Verb patterns.
ec | S | Each of the patients have been given a personal care plan. | Each of the patients has been given a personal care plan. | "Each" takes a singular verb. | Agreement.
ec | S | The unemployment rate has fallen by two per cent from last year. | The unemployment rate has fallen by two per cent since last year. | "Since" introduces the starting point. | Prepositions of time.
ec | S | Scientists have discovered a new species of frog which is thought to be extinct. | Scientists have discovered a species of frog which was thought to be extinct. | The tense must show that the belief was earlier. | Tense consistency.

tr | S | There is little hope that the situation will improve. | UNLIKELY | The situation ___ improve. | is unlikely to | "Unlikely to" expresses low probability.
tr | S | The number of cases has risen sharply. | SHARP | There has been ___ the number of cases. | a sharp rise in | "A sharp rise in" is a fixed collocation.
tr | S | Few people believed the claims of the politicians. | HARDLY | ___ the claims of the politicians. | Hardly anyone believed | "Hardly anyone" means almost nobody.
