# C1 balance batch (articles, passive, pronouns)
es | ART | There was [a] widespread confusion about the new timetable. | widespread | "Confusion" is uncountable, so no article is needed; "a" must go.
es | ART | I would like to offer you [an] advice on this matter. | some | "Advice" is uncountable.
es | ART | The company employs [a] thousands of people worldwide. | thousands of | "A thousands" is ungrammatical.
es | ART | She has [much] friends in the music industry. | many | "Friends" is countable.
es | ART | We had [a] lovely weather during the whole trip. | lovely | "Weather" is uncountable, so no article is used.
es | PASS | The committee is believed to [meet] yesterday. | have met | A past event needs the perfect infinitive.
es | PASS | The cathedral has been [building] since 1990. | built | A passive needs a past participle.
es | PASS | He denied being [tell] about the changes. | told | A passive gerund needs a past participle.
es | PASS | She is said [being] a brilliant surgeon. | to be | The reporting passive takes an infinitive.
es | PASS | The prisoners were being [question] for hours. | questioned | A passive continuous needs a past participle.
fb | ART | The proposal was approved by the board with ___ opposition. | little | few :: "Opposition" is uncountable. | many :: "many" does not work here, because "Opposition" is uncountable. | a few :: "a few" does not work here, because "Opposition" is uncountable. | "Little" goes with uncountable nouns.
fb | ART | There were ___ complaints about the noise, so the council took no action. | few | little :: "Complaints" is countable. | much :: "much" does not work here, because "Complaints" is countable. | a little :: "a little" does not work here, because "Complaints" is countable. | "Few" goes with countable plurals.
fb | ART | ___ the delegates were present at the opening session; a few arrived late. | Most of | Most the :: "Most" needs "of" before "the". | Almost :: "Almost the delegates" is ungrammatical. | Mostly :: "Mostly the delegates" is ungrammatical. | "Most of the" is a standard quantifier.
fb | ART | She spoke with ___ authority on the subject of marine biology. | considerable | considerate :: "Considerate" means thoughtful. | considering :: "Considering" is a participle. | considered :: "Considered" means thought through. | "Considerable" means large.
fb | PASS | The new system is ___ tested at the moment, so please be patient. | being | been :: The continuous passive needs "being". | be :: "be" does not work here, because the continuous passive needs "being". | is :: "Is" is already present. | The present continuous passive uses "being".
fb | PASS | It has been ___ that the minister will resign next week. | rumoured | rumouring :: A passive participle is needed. | rumour :: "Rumour" is a noun. | rumours :: "Rumours" is a noun. | "It has been rumoured that" is a reporting passive.
fb | PASS | The painting is ___ to have been stolen in the 1940s. | thought | think :: A passive participle is needed. | thinking :: "thinking" does not work here, because a passive participle is needed. | thinks :: "thinks" does not work here, because a passive participle is needed. | The reporting passive uses "is thought to".
fb | PRON | The house in ___ she was born was demolished last year. | which | whom :: "Whom" refers to people. | what :: "What" cannot follow a preposition here. | that :: "That" cannot follow a preposition. | "In which" follows the preposition.
fb | PRON | I admire people ___ perseverance has helped them to succeed. | whose | who :: "Who perseverance" is ungrammatical. | whom :: "Whom perseverance" is ungrammatical. | which :: "Which perseverance" does not show possession. | "Whose" shows possession.
vf | ART | Not ___ of the committee members supported the amendment. | all | every :: "Every of" is ungrammatical. | whole :: "Whole of the" has another structure. | each :: "Not each of" is possible but changes the sense. | "Not all of" is a partial negation.
vf | ART | The team made ___ progress in the second half of the season. | steady | steadily :: An adjective is needed. | steadiness :: "Steadiness" is a noun. | steadied :: "Steadied" is a verb form. | "Steady progress" is a standard collocation.
vf | ART | ___ information in the leaflet was out of date. | The | An :: "Information" is uncountable. | Some :: "Some" does not refer to specific information. | A :: "A" does not work here, because "Information" is uncountable. | "The" refers to specific information.
vf | ART | Hardly ___ of the audience stayed until the end. | any | every :: "Hardly every" is ungrammatical. | some :: "Hardly some" is ungrammatical. | all :: "Hardly all" has another sense. | "Hardly any of" means almost none.
vf | PASS | He is thought ___ the country shortly before the scandal broke. | to have left | to leave :: The perfect infinitive refers to an earlier time. | leaving :: A participle does not follow "thought". | having left :: "Thought" takes an infinitive. | The perfect infinitive shows an earlier time.

es | ART | She has made [a] great progress this term. | great | "Progress" is uncountable.
es | PASS | The suspect is thought to [being] abroad. | be | The reporting passive takes a plain infinitive after "to".
