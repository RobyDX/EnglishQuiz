mc | ART | The Prime Minister gave ___ interview to the press after the meeting. | an | the :: "The interview" would imply a known one. | no article :: A singular noun needs a determiner. | "An interview" introduces a new item.
mc | ART | Only ___ handful of people turned up to the lecture. | a | the :: "The handful" would imply a known group. | an :: "Handful" begins with a consonant sound. | "A handful of" means a very small number.
mc | ART | She could not afford ___ luxury of a holiday that year. | the | a :: "A luxury of" would imply any luxury. | no article :: The noun needs a determiner. | "The luxury of" is the idiomatic phrase.
mc | ART | We have ___ little chance of persuading them now. | very | much :: "Much little" is ungrammatical. | so many :: "So many little" is ungrammatical. | "Very little" means almost none.
mc | ART | The team has ___ to be defeated this season. | yet | still :: "Still to be defeated" has another structure. | already :: "Already to be defeated" is ungrammatical. | "Has yet to be defeated" means has not been defeated so far.
mc | ART | By ___ large, the public has welcomed the new measures. | and | or :: "By or large" is not the idiom. | with :: "By with large" is ungrammatical. | "By and large" means generally.
mc | PRON | The two sides could not agree, and ___ would give way. | neither | none :: "None" is used for more than two. | either :: "Either would give way" has another sense. | "Neither would give way" refers to two.
mc | PRON | Anyone ___ wishes to speak should inform the secretary. | who | whom :: The relative acts as the subject. | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | "Who" is the subject of the relative clause.
mc | PRON | Each of the students was asked to bring ___ own dictionary. | his or her | their own's :: This is ungrammatical. | its :: "Its" refers to things. | "His or her" is the formal singular possessive.
mc | PRON | It is not the money ___ matters but the principle. | that | what :: "What" cannot follow a noun. | whom :: "Whom" refers to people. | "That" introduces a cleft clause.
mc | MOD | You ___ well ask why nobody told you earlier. | may | will :: "Will well ask" is ungrammatical. | must :: "Must well" is ungrammatical. | "May well ask" is a fixed phrase.
mc | MOD | She ___ have been about eighty when she died, but nobody knows for sure. | may | will :: "Will have been" expresses a prediction. | shall :: "Shall have been" is archaic. | "May have been" expresses a possibility.
mc | MOD | The company ___ to have been warned of the risks, but did nothing. | ought | should to :: "Should" is not followed by "to". | must to :: "Must" is not followed by "to". | "Ought to have been warned" expresses an expectation.
mc | MOD | I ___ think of nothing worse than being stuck in a lift. | can | will :: "Will think of" is a prediction. | shall :: "Shall think of" is archaic. | "Can think of nothing worse" is a fixed phrase.
mc | MOD | They ___ have left earlier if they had known about the roadworks. | would | will :: "Will" does not fit an unreal past. | could to :: "Could to" is ungrammatical. | "Would have left" completes the conditional.
mc | MOD | We ___ be more delighted with the result. | could not | cannot to :: "Cannot to" is ungrammatical. | would to :: "Would to" is ungrammatical. | "Could not be more delighted" is an emphatic idiom.

fb | ADJ | The proposal was ___ , in that it satisfied none of the parties fully. | unsatisfactory | unsatisfied :: "Unsatisfied" describes people. | unsatisfying :: "Unsatisfying" is possible but weaker. | "Unsatisfactory" describes the proposal.
fb | ADJ | The weather turned ___ in the afternoon, and we abandoned the climb. | treacherous | treasonous :: "Treasonous" means disloyal. | tremendous :: "Tremendous" means very great. | "Treacherous" means dangerous.

fb | ART | We spent a ___ deal of time discussing the budget. | good | well :: "A well deal" is ungrammatical. | nice :: "A nice deal of time" is not standard. | "A good deal of" means a lot of.

fb | PRON | The Prime Minister, ___ was visibly tired, left the conference early. | who | whom :: The relative acts as the subject. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | "Who" is the subject of the relative clause.
fb | PRON | The reasons for ___ the decision was taken remain secret. | which | what :: "What" cannot follow a preposition here. | that :: "That" cannot follow a preposition. | "For which" follows the preposition.
fb | PRON | Whatever the outcome, we shall have done ___ best. | our | ours :: "Ours" cannot precede a noun. | us :: "Us" is an object pronoun. | "Our best" is a possessive determiner.
fb | PRON | She has two cars, ___ of which is more than ten years old. | neither | none :: "None" is used for more than two. | any :: "Any of which" changes the sense. | "Neither of which" refers to two.
fb | PRON | It is the way he speaks to people ___ annoys me. | that | what :: "What" cannot follow a noun. | whom :: "Whom" refers to people. | "That" introduces a cleft clause.
fb | PRON | The prize will be shared between ___ of the finalists. | all | whole :: "Whole" is used with singular nouns. | every :: "Every" takes a singular noun. | "All of the" goes with a plural noun.
fb | MOD | He ___ not have known how serious the situation was, or he would have acted. | can | will to :: "Will to" is ungrammatical. | may to :: "May to" is ungrammatical. | "Cannot have known" is a negative deduction.
fb | MOD | The directors ___ have foreseen the consequences of their decision, but they did not. | should | would to :: "Would to" is ungrammatical. | must :: "Must have foreseen" is a deduction. | "Should have foreseen" expresses criticism.
fb | MOD | I'll ring you tomorrow ___ I be free. | should | shall :: "Shall I be free" is archaic. | would :: "Would I be free" has another sense. | "Should I be free" is a formal conditional inversion.
fb | MOD | We ___ do with some extra help over the weekend. | could | must :: "Must do with" is ungrammatical. | would to :: "Would to" is ungrammatical. | "Could do with" means would benefit from.
fb | MOD | They ___ get here before noon if the traffic is light. | might | must :: "Must" expresses certainty. | would to :: "Would to" is ungrammatical. | "Might" expresses a possibility.
