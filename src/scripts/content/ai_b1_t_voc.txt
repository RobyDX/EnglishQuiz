mc | T | Our flight was ___ for two hours because of fog. | delayed | cancelled :: "Cancelled" means it did not happen. | postponed :: "Postponed" is used for events. | stopped :: "Stopped" does not collocate with flights. | "Delayed" means late.
mc | T | Could you tell me where the ___ is? I need to catch the 9.30 train. | platform | pavement :: A pavement is a path for walkers. | passage :: A passage is a narrow way. | station hall :: This is not the exact place. | "Platform" is where passengers wait for trains.
mc | T | Is the price ___ of breakfast? | inclusive | including :: "Including" does not take "of". | included :: "Included" is used without "of". | exclusive :: It means the opposite. | "Inclusive of" means that something is included.
mc | T | Remember to ___ your passport before you leave the hotel. | take | bring :: "Bring" is towards the speaker. | carry on :: "Carry on" means continue. | wear :: You do not wear a passport. | "Take" is used for leaving.
mc | T | The beach was so ___ that we could hardly find a place to sit. | crowded | busy up :: "Busy up" is not used. | full up :: "Full up" is informal. | filled :: "Filled" is not the usual adjective. | "Crowded" means full of people.
mc | T | Visitors are not ___ to take photographs inside the museum. | allowed | permitted to :: "Permitted to" would repeat "to". | let :: "Let" does not take a passive. | agreed :: "Agreed" does not fit. | "Are not allowed to" is the passive form.
mc | T | If you want to save money, travel in the ___ season. | low | cheap :: "Cheap season" is not used. | little :: "Little season" is not used. | small :: "Small season" is not used. | "Low season" is the standard collocation.
mc | T | After a long journey we were glad to ___ our luggage in the room. | unpack | uncover :: "Uncover" means to remove a cover. | undo :: "Undo" means to reverse. | unload up :: "Unload up" is not used. | "Unpack" means to take things out of bags.
mc | T | The tour bus ___ at the main square at ten o'clock. | departs | reaches :: "Reaches" does not fit the starting point. | leaves for :: "Leaves for" needs a destination. | goes up :: "Goes up" has a different meaning. | "Departs" means leaves.
mc | T | This ticket is ___ for one month. | valid | useful :: "Useful" is not about time. | active :: "Active" is not usual for tickets. | true :: "True" is not used for tickets. | "Valid" means usable.
fb | T | Please ___ in at least two hours before your flight. | check | go :: "Go in" does not mean register. | sign :: "Sign in" is used online. | put :: "Put in" has a different meaning. | "Check in" means to register.
fb | T | I'd love to go ___ a trip around the world. | on | at :: "At a trip" is not used. | for :: "For a trip" has another meaning. | in :: "In a trip" is not used. | "Go on a trip".
sc | T | We stayed at a hotel near the airport. | We stayed in a hotel nearly the airport. :: "Nearly" is an adverb. | We stayed at hotel near the airport. :: An article is needed. | "Near the airport".
sc | T | The plane took off an hour late. | The plane took out an hour late. :: "Took out" has another meaning. | The plane took up an hour late. :: "Took up" has another meaning. | "Take off" is used for planes.
sc | T | I'm looking forward to my holiday. | I'm looking forward my holiday. :: "To" is missing. | I'm looking forward for my holiday. :: "Forward" takes "to". | "Look forward to".
sc | T | They arrived in London on Tuesday. | They arrived to London on Tuesday. :: "Arrive" does not take "to". | They arrived at London on Tuesday. :: "Arrive at" is for small places. | "Arrive in" + city.
sc | T | The hotel was much better than I expected. | The hotel was more better than I expected. :: "Better" is already a comparative. | The hotel was much good than I expected. :: The comparative is needed. | "Much better than".
sc | T | We've been travelling for three weeks. | We are travelling for three weeks. :: "For three weeks" needs a perfect form. | We have travel for three weeks. :: After "have" use a participle. | "Have been travelling".
sc | T | Passengers must show their passports at the gate. | Passengers must to show their passports at the gate. :: "Must" is followed by the base form. | Passengers must showing their passports at the gate. :: This version is wrong in another way: "Must" is followed by the base form. | "Must show".
sc | T | If I had more time, I would visit the museum. | If I would have more time, I would visit the museum. :: The if-clause uses the past simple. | If I have more time, I would visit the museum. :: This mixes conditionals. | Second conditional.
sc | T | The guide asked us not to touch the paintings. | The guide asked us to not touching the paintings. :: The negative infinitive is "not to touch". | The guide asked us don't touch the paintings. :: A reported command uses the infinitive. | "Not to touch".
