# C1 grammar batch 2: mc, fb, vf, vc, es for articles, pronouns, prepositions, modals, tenses, vocabulary
mc | ART | The jury found the evidence ___ and acquitted the defendant. | inconclusive | inconclusively :: An adjective is needed after "found ... ". | inconclude :: This word does not exist. | inclusion :: "Inclusion" is a noun. | "Inconclusive" means not decisive.
mc | ART | ___ people believe that technology will solve every environmental problem, but few experts agree. | Some | Any :: "Any people believe" is ungrammatical. | Every :: "Every people" is ungrammatical. | Much :: "Much people" is ungrammatical. | "Some" is used with plural countable nouns.
mc | ART | There was ___ little enthusiasm for the proposal that it was quietly withdrawn. | so | such :: "Such little enthusiasm" is awkward. | very :: "Very little ... that" needs "so". | too :: "Too" does not introduce a result with "that". | "So little ... that" shows a result.
mc | ART | We cannot afford to waste ___ more time on this issue. | any | some :: "Some" is not used in negatives like this. | a :: "A more time" is ungrammatical. | many :: "Time" is uncountable. | "Any more" is used after a negative.
mc | ART | It was ___ fascinating exhibition that we stayed until the gallery closed. | such a | so a :: "So a" is not the standard order. | quite :: "Quite fascinating exhibition" lacks an article. | very :: "Very" cannot introduce a result. | "Such a + adjective + noun + that" shows a result.
mc | ART | Hardly ___ of the staff attended the training session. | any | no :: "Hardly no" is a double negative. | some :: "Hardly some" is not idiomatic. | all :: "Hardly all" changes the meaning. | "Hardly any" means almost none.
mc | ART | The professor gave ___ lecture on the origins of language that held the audience spellbound. | a fascinating | the fascinating :: The lecture has not been mentioned before. | fascinating :: A singular noun needs an article. | an fascinating :: "Fascinating" begins with a consonant sound. | "A" introduces a singular countable noun.
mc | ART | She has ___ knowledge of the subject than any of her colleagues. | more | much :: "Much knowledge than" is ungrammatical. | most :: "Most than" is ungrammatical. | many :: "Knowledge" is uncountable. | "More ... than" forms a comparative.
mc | PRON | The report, ___ authors were all independent experts, criticised the government's approach. | whose | which :: "Which authors" does not show possession. | whom :: "Whom authors" is ungrammatical. | who :: "Who authors" is ungrammatical. | "Whose" shows possession.
mc | PRON | I'd like to thank everyone ___ helped to make the event a success. | who | whom :: "Whom" is an object form. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | that's :: "That's" means "that is". | "Who" is the subject.
mc | PRON | It is not the cost ___ worries me, but the delay. | that | what :: "What" cannot follow a noun. | which :: "Which" is less natural in a cleft. | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | "That" introduces a cleft clause.
mc | PRON | He gave me the impression of ___ who had never doubted his own abilities. | someone | one :: "One who" would be formal but "someone" fits the structure. | whom :: "Whom" is not a pronoun of this kind. | himself :: "Himself" is reflexive. | "Someone who" describes a type of person.
mc | PRON | She had no intention of showing ___ the letter. | anyone | everyone :: "No intention of showing everyone" changes the meaning. | someone :: "Someone" is not used in negatives. | no one :: "No intention of showing no one" is a double negative. | "Anyone" is used in negative contexts.
mc | PRON | Whoever ___ the manager's office must first report to reception. | enters | enter :: "Whoever" takes a singular verb. | entering :: A finite verb is needed. | entered :: The sentence states a rule. | "Whoever" takes a singular present verb.
mc | PREP | The company is seeking to diversify ___ new markets overseas. | into | to :: "Diversify to" is not standard. | at :: "Diversify at" is not standard. | by :: "Diversify by" is not standard. | "Diversify into" is the standard collocation.
mc | PREP | The inquiry was set up ___ the aftermath of the disaster. | in | on :: "On the aftermath" is not standard. | at :: "At the aftermath" is not standard. | by :: "By the aftermath" is not standard. | "In the aftermath of" is the fixed phrase.
mc | PREP | The two companies have agreed to merge ___ the condition that jobs are protected. | on | in :: "In the condition" is not standard. | under :: "Under the condition" is possible but "on" is the fixed phrase. | with :: "With the condition" is not standard. | "On the condition that" is the fixed phrase.
mc | PREP | She is renowned ___ her ability to explain complex ideas simply. | for | of :: "Renowned of" is not standard. | by :: "Renowned by" is not standard. | with :: "Renowned with" is not standard. | "Renowned for" introduces the reason for fame.
mc | PREP | It is difficult to see how the scheme could be made to work ___ extra funding. | without | except :: "Except extra funding" is possible but less natural. | lacking :: "Lacking" would need "in". | free :: "Free extra funding" has a different meaning. | "Without" expresses absence.
mc | MOD | You ___ not be aware of it, but the system has been down for the past three hours. | may | shall :: "Shall not be aware" is not natural. | ought :: "Ought not be aware" lacks "to". | would :: "Would not be aware" changes the meaning. | "May not" expresses possibility.
mc | MOD | We ___ to have left earlier; now we will miss the train. | ought | should to :: "Should" is not followed by "to". | must :: "Must to have" is ungrammatical. | would :: "Would to have" is ungrammatical. | "Ought to have" expresses regret.
mc | MOD | He ___ well be right, but I still think we should check. | may | would :: "Would well" is not idiomatic. | shall :: "Shall well" is not idiomatic. | ought :: "Ought well be" lacks "to". | "May well" expresses a strong possibility.
mc | VT | The negotiations ___ for weeks before an agreement was finally reached. | had been continuing | have been continuing :: The present perfect does not fit a past agreement. | continued :: The simple past does not show duration before the agreement. | were continued :: The negotiations are the doers. | The past perfect continuous shows duration before a past event.
mc | VT | She told me that she ___ the manuscript by the end of the week. | would have finished | will have finished :: Reported speech shifts "will" to "would". | has finished :: The present perfect does not fit. | finished :: The future perfect shifts back. | "Will have finished" becomes "would have finished" in reported speech.
fb | ART | ___ the evidence suggests that the policy has failed, ministers refuse to admit it. | Although | Despite :: "Despite" is followed by a noun or -ing. | However :: "However" is an adverb. | Because :: "Because" gives the wrong meaning. | "Although" introduces a concession clause.
fb | ART | The company has ___ little chance of surviving without fresh investment. | very | a :: "A little chance" would be positive. | so :: "So little chance" needs a result clause. | too :: "Too little chance" needs "to" or "for". | "Very little" emphasises the scarcity.
fb | ART | Not ___ the findings were surprising, but several were clearly at odds with previous research. | all | every :: "Not every the findings" is ungrammatical. | each :: "Not each the findings" is ungrammatical. | whole :: "Not whole" is ungrammatical. | "Not all" is a partial negation.
fb | ART | By ___ the most important factor in the decision was cost. | far | much :: "By much the" is not standard. | long :: "By long the" is not standard. | a lot :: "By a lot the" is not standard. | "By far the most" intensifies a superlative.
fb | PRON | The man ___ I mistook for the manager turned out to be a customer. | whom | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | what :: "What" cannot follow a noun. | "Whom" is the object relative pronoun.
fb | PRON | Those ___ have not yet registered should do so before the end of the week. | who | whom :: "Whom" is an object form. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | "Those who" refers to people in general.
fb | PRON | I could hardly believe my ___ when I saw the result. | eyes | eye :: "Believe my eye" is not the idiom. | sight :: "Believe my sight" is not the idiom. | looks :: "Believe my looks" is not the idiom. | "Could hardly believe my eyes" is a fixed idiom.
fb | PREP | He is not ___ to losing his temper when provoked. | prone | proud :: "Proud to" has a different meaning. | prompt :: "Prompt to" has a different meaning. | pronounced :: "Pronounced to" is ungrammatical. | "Prone to" means likely to experience.
fb | PREP | She was relieved ___ discover that the exam had been postponed. | to | at :: "Relieved at discover" is ungrammatical. | for :: "Relieved for discover" is ungrammatical. | of :: "Relieved of discover" is ungrammatical. | "Relieved to" is followed by an infinitive.
fb | PREP | The decision has far-reaching implications ___ the whole industry. | for | at :: "Implications at" is not standard. | to :: "Implications to" is less usual. | of :: "Implications of" would introduce the source. | "Implications for" introduces what is affected.
fb | VT | By the time you receive this letter, I ___ the country. | will have left | will leave :: The future perfect shows completion. | leave :: A present tense does not fit. | have left :: The present perfect does not fit a future time. | "By the time" + future perfect.
vf | ART | The committee has ___ authority to approve spending of this size. | no | not :: "Not authority" is ungrammatical. | none :: "None authority" is ungrammatical. | any :: "Has any authority" does not express denial. | "No" means not any.
vf | PRON | There is little that ___ can do to alter the outcome. | anyone | everyone :: "Little that everyone can do" changes the meaning. | someone :: "Someone" is not used in negatives. | no one :: "No one" would give a double negative. | "Anyone" is used with a negative sense.
vf | PRON | It was Maria ___ first noticed the error. | who | whom :: "Whom" is an object form. | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | "Who" is the subject of the cleft clause.
vf | PREP | They have been engaged ___ a bitter dispute for months. | in | at :: "Engaged at" is not standard. | on :: "Engaged on" is not standard. | with :: "Engaged with" has a different meaning. | "Engaged in" a dispute is the standard collocation.
vf | MOD | ___ I known what was going to happen, I would have stayed at home. | Had | Have :: The inverted third conditional uses "had". | Did :: "Did" does not form this conditional. | Would :: "Would I known" is ungrammatical. | "Had" + subject + participle is the inverted third conditional.
vc | ART | She did not show ___ (any) interest in the proposal. | any | no :: "Did not show no" is a double negative. | some :: "Some" is not used in negatives like this. | much of :: "Much of" changes the structure. | "Any" is used after a negative verb.
vc | PRON | The people ___ (with whom / I / work) are extremely supportive. | with whom I work | whom I work :: The preposition is missing. | who I work them :: The pronoun is repeated. | which I work with :: "Which" refers to things. | "With whom I work" is the formal pattern.
vc | VT | She ___ (live) in Rome for ten years before she moved to Paris. | had lived | has lived :: The past perfect shows an earlier period. | lived :: The simple past does not show the sequence. | was living :: The continuous does not show completion. | The past perfect shows a period before another past event.
es | ART | I have [little] friends who share my interest in birdwatching. | few | "Friends" is countable.
es | PRON | The writer, [who] books I admire, will give a talk. | whose | "Whose" shows possession.
es | PREP | They were accused [for] corruption and fraud. | of | "Accused of" introduces the alleged offence.
