# C1 articles, determiners and quantifiers
mc | ART | ___ evidence presented at the trial was circumstantial, and the jury remained unconvinced. | The | An :: "Evidence" is uncountable and cannot take "an". | Some of :: "Some of" needs a following determiner. | A :: "Evidence" is uncountable and cannot take "a". | "The evidence" refers to specific evidence introduced at the trial.
mc | ART | There was ___ of the early enthusiasm left by the time the project entered its third year. | little | few :: "Enthusiasm" is uncountable, so "few" cannot be used. | a few :: "A few" goes with countable nouns. | many :: "Many" goes with countable nouns. | "Little" with an uncountable noun means almost none.
mc | ART | The new law will affect ___ one of us, regardless of age or income. | every | all :: "All one of us" is not grammatical. | each of :: "Each of one" duplicates the quantifier. | whole :: "Whole one of us" is not grammatical. | "Every one of us" emphasises that no individual is excluded.
mc | ART | Hardly ___ of the delegates had read the report before the meeting began. | any | no :: "Hardly no" is a double negative. | some :: "Hardly some" is not idiomatic. | much :: "Much" cannot go with the countable "delegates". | "Hardly any" means very few.
mc | ART | She is ___ authority on medieval manuscripts and is frequently consulted by museums. | an | the :: "The authority" would suggest she is the only one. | – :: A singular noun needs a determiner. | some :: "Some" does not fit a single expert. | "An authority on" introduces one of several experts.
mc | ART | ___ of the two proposals addresses the underlying problem, so we must start again. | Neither | None :: "None" is for three or more. | Either :: "Either" would suggest at least one does. | Both :: "Both" would suggest each addresses it. | "Neither" refers to two items and is negative.
mc | ART | Not ___ the candidates were equally qualified, but all were given a fair hearing. | all | every :: "Not every the candidates" is ungrammatical. | each :: "Not each the candidates" is ungrammatical. | whole :: "Whole" cannot be used this way. | "Not all the candidates" is a partial negation.
mc | ART | We are doing everything in our power to ensure that ___ money is wasted. | no | none :: "None money" is ungrammatical. | not :: "Not money" is ungrammatical. | any :: "Any money is wasted" would not mean the same. | "No" is the determiner meaning not any.
mc | ART | She spoke with ___ conviction that even her critics were impressed. | such | so :: "So conviction" is ungrammatical. | too :: "Too conviction" is ungrammatical. | very :: "Very conviction" is ungrammatical. | "Such + noun + that" shows a result.
mc | ART | It was ___ a remarkable performance that the audience gave a standing ovation. | such | so :: "So a remarkable performance" is not the standard order. | quite so :: "Quite so" does not fit here. | very :: "Very" cannot combine with "a remarkable performance" like this. | "Such a + adjective + noun" is followed by a result clause.
mc | ART | ___ number of complaints has risen sharply since the new system was introduced. | The | A :: "A number of" takes a plural verb, but "has" is singular. | Some :: "Some number of" is not idiomatic here. | Many :: "Many number" is ungrammatical. | "The number of" takes a singular verb.
mc | ART | ___ number of students have expressed concern about the new assessment procedures. | A | The :: "The number of" takes a singular verb. | Some of :: "Some of number" is ungrammatical. | Each :: "Each number" does not fit. | "A number of" means several and takes a plural verb.
mc | ART | The management, ___ of whom had been with the firm for decades, resisted any change. | most | the most :: "The most of whom" is not idiomatic. | almost :: "Almost of whom" is ungrammatical. | mostly :: "Mostly of whom" is ungrammatical. | "Most of whom" is the standard relative construction.
mc | ART | By ___ large, the survey confirmed what we had already suspected. | and | the :: "By the large" is not the idiom. | a :: "By a large" is not the idiom. | far :: "By far large" is not the idiom. | "By and large" means generally.
mc | ART | He claimed to have no recollection of ___ meeting in question. | the | a :: "A meeting in question" is not idiomatic. | that :: "That meeting in question" is awkward. | – :: A singular countable noun needs a determiner. | "The ... in question" identifies a specific item.
mc | ART | ___ more she thought about it, the less convinced she became. | The | A :: "A more" does not form the correlative. | Much :: "Much more" does not combine with "the less". | Some :: "Some more" does not form the correlative. | "The more ... the less" is a double comparative.
mc | ART | I haven't the faintest ___ what she meant by that remark. | idea | ideas :: "The faintest idea" is singular. | notion about :: "Notion about" does not fit the idiom. | thought :: "Thought" does not fit the idiom. | "Haven't the faintest idea" is a fixed idiom.
fb | ART | The two sides were ___ willing to compromise, so talks collapsed. | neither | either :: "Either willing" suggests at least one was willing. | none :: "None" is for more than two. | both :: "Both willing" contradicts the result. | "Neither" refers to two and is negative.
fb | ART | ___ small the risk, it should not be ignored. | However | Whatever :: "Whatever small" is ungrammatical. | Whichever :: "Whichever small" is ungrammatical. | Although :: "Although small the risk" has the wrong word order. | "However + adjective" means no matter how.
fb | ART | We discussed ___ of the options, but none seemed practical. | several | much :: "Options" is countable. | little :: "little" does not work here, because "Options" is countable. | every :: "Every of" is ungrammatical. | "Several of the options" refers to a number of items.
fb | ART | I have ___ objection to the plan, provided that costs are kept down. | no | not :: "Not objection" is ungrammatical. | none :: "None objection" is ungrammatical. | any :: "I have any objection" is ungrammatical. | "No objection" means not any objection.
fb | ART | ___ people, if any, would have noticed the error without being told. | Few | A few :: "A few" suggests some would have noticed. | Little :: "Little" goes with uncountable nouns. | Much :: "Much" goes with uncountable nouns. | "Few" means almost none.
fb | ART | The company has made ___ progress this year, though more remains to be done. | considerable | considerate :: "Considerate" means thoughtful. | considering :: "Considering" is a participle. | considerably :: "Considerably" is an adverb. | "Considerable progress" means a large amount.
fb | ART | They were both ___ of the dangers of the expedition. | aware | awareness :: "Awareness" is a noun. | awarely :: This word does not exist. | awake :: "Awake" means not sleeping. | "Aware of" is the fixed collocation.
vf | ART | As ___ result of the delay, several flights were rescheduled. | a | the :: "The result" would require a specific, known result. | an :: "Result" begins with a consonant sound. | – :: A singular noun needs a determiner. | "As a result of" is a fixed phrase.
vf | ART | There is ___ little evidence that the treatment is effective. | very | a :: "A little evidence" would be positive. | so :: "So little evidence" needs a result clause. | too :: "Too little evidence" needs "for" or "to". | "Very little" emphasises the scarcity.
vf | ART | The report, ___ findings were widely publicised, led to a change in policy. | whose | which :: "Which findings" cannot show possession here. | what :: "What findings" is ungrammatical. | its :: "Its findings were" cannot join the clause directly. | "Whose" shows possession.
vf | ART | He gave ___ the impression of someone who had nothing to hide. | us | to us :: "Give us the impression" does not take "to". | for us :: "For us" is not idiomatic here. | with us :: "With us" is not idiomatic here. | "Give someone the impression" takes an indirect object directly.
vf | ART | Some of the manuscripts, ___ were centuries old, had been badly damaged. | which | what :: "What" cannot introduce a relative clause. | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | that :: "That" cannot follow a comma. | "Which" introduces a nonrestrictive clause.
vf | ART | Each of the contestants ___ asked to introduce themselves. | was | were :: "Each" takes a singular verb. | are :: "are" does not work here, because "Each" takes a singular verb. | being :: A finite verb is needed. | "Each" takes a singular verb.
vc | ART | I have ___ (few / doubt) that she will succeed. | little | few :: "Doubt" is uncountable. | a few :: "a few" does not work here, because "Doubt" is uncountable. | many :: "many" does not work here, because "Doubt" is uncountable. | "Little doubt" means almost no doubt.
es | ART | The government is spending [many] money on the campaign. | much | "Money" is uncountable.
es | ART | [All] of the two candidates withdrew. | Both | "All" is used for more than two.
es | ART | She did not make [a] progress this term. | any | "Progress" is uncountable.
es | ART | There is [less] people than I expected. | fewer | "Fewer" goes with countable nouns.
es | ART | [A] few of the evidence was convincing. | Little | "Evidence" is uncountable.
es | ART | They spent [a] whole of the night arguing. | the | "The whole" is the standard order.
ec | ART | The amount of complaints has increased significantly over the past year. | The number of complaints has increased significantly over the past year. | "Complaints" is countable, so "number" is used. | Number and amount.
ec | ART | He is one of those people who never admits they are wrong. | He is one of those people who never admit they are wrong. | The verb agrees with the plural "people". | Agreement in relative clauses.
ec | ART | Neither of the proposals were acceptable. | Neither of the proposals was acceptable. | In careful usage "neither" takes a singular verb. | Agreement.
ec | ART | Each employees must sign the document. | Each employee must sign the document. | "Each" is followed by a singular noun. | Each.
ec | ART | The information they gave us were incomplete. | The information they gave us was incomplete. | "Information" is uncountable and takes a singular verb. | Agreement.
ec | ART | There is a lot of ways of solving the problem. | There are a lot of ways of solving the problem. | The verb agrees with the plural "ways". | Agreement.
ec | ART | Less than twenty candidates applied for the post. | Fewer than twenty candidates applied for the post. | "Fewer" goes with countable nouns. | Fewer and less.
ec | ART | He made a lot of progresses during the course. | He made a lot of progress during the course. | "Progress" is uncountable. | Uncountable nouns.
