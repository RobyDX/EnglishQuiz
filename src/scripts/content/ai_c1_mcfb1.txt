# C1 mc/fb batch: collocations, idioms and vocabulary
mc | E | She has an extraordinary ___ for spotting talent before anyone else notices it. | eye | ear :: "An ear for" is used with music or languages. | nose :: "A nose for" is used with scandal or trouble. | hand :: "A hand for" is not the usual collocation. | "An eye for" means a talent for noticing.
mc | E | After the argument, the two friends decided to bury the ___ and start again. | hatchet | axe :: "Bury the axe" is not the idiom. | sword :: "Bury the sword" is not the idiom. | knife :: "Bury the knife" is not the idiom. | "Bury the hatchet" means to end a quarrel.
mc | E | His promises turned out to be nothing more than ___ air; he never delivered on any of them. | hot | thin :: "Thin air" means nothing, but "hot air" means empty talk. | fresh :: "Fresh air" is literal. | cold :: "Cold air" is literal. | "Hot air" means empty, boastful talk.
mc | E | The old house had a ___ atmosphere that made visitors lower their voices. | hushed | hush :: "Hush" is a verb or noun. | hushing :: "Hushing" is a participle. | hushes :: "Hushes" is a verb form. | "Hushed" means quiet and still.
mc | E | They have been at ___ over the inheritance ever since their mother's funeral. | odds | ends :: "At ends" is not the idiom. | war :: "At war over" is possible but "at odds" is the idiom for disagreement. | sea :: "At sea" means confused or on a ship. | "At odds" means in disagreement.
mc | E | She tried to ___ her disappointment, but her voice betrayed her. | mask | masque :: A "masque" is a dramatic entertainment. | mash :: "Mash" means to crush. | mast :: A "mast" is a pole on a ship. | "Mask" means to hide.
mc | E | It took her a long time to come to ___ with the loss of her job. | terms | grips :: "Come to grips with" has a different structure. | ends :: "Come to ends" is not the idiom. | peace :: "Come to peace" is not the idiom. | "Come to terms with" means to accept.
mc | E | She had a ___ feeling that something was about to go wrong. | nagging | nagged :: "Nagged" is a past tense. | nag :: "Nag" is a verb or noun. | nags :: "Nags" is a verb form. | "Nagging" means persistently worrying.
mc | E | I am afraid I cannot ___ with such rudeness any longer. | put up | put on :: "Put on" means to wear. | put out :: "Put out" means to extinguish. | put in :: "Put in" means to insert. | "Put up with" means to tolerate.
mc | E | She was ___ with grief and unable to speak at her husband's funeral. | overcome | overcame :: A participle is needed. | overcoming :: "Overcoming" is a participle. | overcomes :: "Overcomes" is a verb form. | "Overcome with" means overwhelmed.
mc | E | He is a ___ individual who has never followed the crowd. | maverick | meander :: "Meander" means to wander. | mercenary :: A "mercenary" fights for money. | martyr :: A "martyr" suffers for a cause. | A "maverick" is an independent-minded person.
mc | T | The cruise ship set ___ from Southampton at noon, bound for the Caribbean. | sail | out :: "Set out" is not used for ships leaving port in this phrase. | off :: "Set off" is used for starting journeys in general. | forth :: "Set forth" is archaic. | "Set sail" is the idiom for a ship starting a voyage.
mc | T | The tourist board has launched a campaign to ___ the region's cultural heritage. | promote | prompt :: "Prompt" means to cause. | prohibit :: "Prohibit" means to ban. | provoke :: "Provoke" means to cause a reaction. | "Promote" means to publicise.
mc | T | Visitors can expect a warm ___ from the locals, who pride themselves on their hospitality. | welcome | welcomed :: "Welcomed" is a participle. | welcoming :: "Welcoming" is an adjective. | welcomes :: "Welcomes" is a verb form. | "A warm welcome" is a fixed collocation.
mc | W | The proposal has been met with ___ from several quarters, and it may have to be revised. | criticism | critic :: A "critic" is a person. | critical :: "Critical" is an adjective. | criticise :: "Criticise" is a verb. | "Criticism" is the noun.
mc | W | The firm's reputation ___ on its ability to deliver on time and within budget. | rests | rest :: "Rest" does not agree with a singular subject. | resting :: An auxiliary is missing. | rested :: The sentence describes a general truth. | "Rests on" means depends on.
mc | W | She has been working ___ the clock to meet the deadline. | against | around :: "Around the clock" has a different meaning. | beyond :: "Beyond the clock" is not the idiom. | over :: "Over the clock" is not the idiom. | "Against the clock" means in a hurry because of limited time.
mc | W | The new manager is determined to ___ some order into the chaos of the department. | bring | take :: "Take order into" is not the idiom. | put :: "Put order into" is not the idiom. | make :: "Make order into" is not the idiom. | "Bring order into chaos" is a natural collocation.
mc | W | Despite repeated warnings, the company ___ ahead with its controversial restructuring plan. | pressed | pushed :: "Pushed ahead" is possible but "pressed ahead" is the idiom. | pulled :: "Pulled ahead" means overtook. | pitched :: "Pitched ahead" is not standard. | "Press ahead" means to proceed with determination.
mc | W | The report calls for a radical ___ of the education system. | overhaul | overhead :: "Overhead" means above. | overlook :: "Overlook" means to fail to notice. | overhear :: "Overhear" means to hear by chance. | An "overhaul" is a thorough revision.
mc | S | The scheme was designed to ___ the gap between the richest and poorest regions. | narrow | narrate :: "Narrate" means to tell a story. | narrowly :: "Narrowly" is an adverb. | narrows :: After "to" use the base form. | "Narrow the gap" means to reduce it.
mc | S | The committee was set up to ___ allegations of misconduct by police officers. | investigate | investigated :: After "to" use the base form. | investment :: "Investment" is a noun. | investigator :: An "investigator" is a person. | "Investigate" means to examine.
fb | E | Her ___ of humour made her popular with colleagues and clients alike. | sense | sensibility :: "Sensibility" means sensitivity. | sensation :: "Sensation" is a feeling. | sensor :: A "sensor" is a device. | "Sense of humour" is a fixed phrase.
fb | E | He ___ to the temptation of checking his phone during the meeting. | succumbed | succeeded :: "Succeeded" means achieved. | succeed :: A past form is needed. | succumbing :: An auxiliary is missing. | "Succumb to" means to yield.
fb | E | The film was a ___ of emotions, moving the audience to laughter and tears alike. | roller-coaster | roller skate :: A "roller skate" is a shoe. | rolling pin :: A "rolling pin" is a kitchen tool. | roundabout :: A "roundabout" is a junction. | A "roller-coaster of emotions" is a fixed phrase.
fb | T | The coach tour was a whirlwind ___ of the major European capitals. | tour | trip :: A "trip" is a single journey. | travel :: "Travel" is uncountable. | trek :: A "trek" is a long hard walk. | A "whirlwind tour" is a quick visit to many places.
fb | T | We stumbled ___ a charming little restaurant in a side street. | across | along :: "Stumbled along" means walked unsteadily. | over :: "Stumbled over" means tripped. | into :: "Stumbled into" is possible but "across" suggests finding by chance. | "Stumble across" means to find by chance.
fb | T | The journey, which ought to have taken two hours, ___ a whole day because of the floods. | took | made :: "Made a whole day" is not natural. | lasted :: "Lasted a whole day" is possible but "took" matches the structure. | held :: "Held a whole day" is not natural. | "It took" expresses the time needed.
fb | T | Passengers are urged to ___ in good time to avoid the queues at security. | check in | check up :: "Check up" means to examine. | check on :: "Check on" means to inspect. | check out :: "Check out" means to leave a hotel. | "Check in" means to register at an airport.
fb | W | The company has ___ a strict code of conduct for all its employees. | adopted | adapted :: "Adapted" means modified. | adept :: "Adept" is an adjective. | adopt :: A participle is needed. | "Adopt" means to take up officially.
fb | W | We need to ___ a decision as soon as possible; the deadline is approaching fast. | reach | arrive :: "Arrive a decision" is not a collocation. | come :: "Come a decision" is not a collocation. | get :: "Get a decision" is informal. | "Reach a decision" is the standard collocation.
fb | W | The negotiators were unable to ___ common ground on the question of pay. | find | catch :: "Catch common ground" is not the collocation. | make :: "Make common ground" is not the collocation. | take :: "Take common ground" is not the collocation. | "Find common ground" means to reach agreement.
vf | E | It is high time you ___ the truth about what happened. | told | tell :: After "it is high time" a past form is used. | will tell :: "Will" is not used here. | have told :: The present perfect does not fit. | "High time" + past simple states that action is overdue.
vf | T | The itinerary, ___ had been agreed months earlier, was changed at the last moment. | which | what :: "What" cannot introduce a relative clause. | that :: "That" cannot follow a comma. | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | "Which" introduces a nonrestrictive clause.
vf | W | She is regarded ___ one of the most gifted engineers of her generation. | as | like :: "Regarded like" is not standard. | to :: "Regarded to" is not standard. | for :: "Regarded for" is not standard. | "Regard as" is the fixed pattern.
vc | W | The report ___ (publish) next week will reveal the full extent of the losses. | to be published | published :: "To be published" shows a future event. | publishing :: "Publishing" is active. | being published :: "Being published" shows an action in progress. | An infinitive passive refers to a planned event.
es | E | She was deeply [touching] by his generosity. | touched | "Touched" describes how she felt.
es | S | There has been a growing [concerned] about rising prices. | concern | A noun is needed.
