# C1 batch 7: fill-the-gap and error-spot, vocabulary and structures
fb | E | Her speech was ___ with emotion, and many in the audience were moved to tears. | charged | changed :: "Changed" means altered. | chased :: "Chased" means pursued. | charted :: "Charted" means mapped. | "Charged with emotion" means full of feeling.
fb | E | The two sisters had a ___ relationship, alternating between affection and bitter quarrels. | turbulent | turbine :: A "turbine" is a machine. | turbid :: "Turbid" describes muddy liquid. | turgid :: "Turgid" means swollen or pompous. | "Turbulent" means stormy.
fb | E | After the divorce, she had to ___ her life and begin again in a new city. | rebuild | rebuke :: "Rebuke" means to scold. | rebound :: "Rebound" means to bounce back. | rebel :: "Rebel" means to resist. | "Rebuild" means to build again.
fb | E | She gave a ___ account of her childhood, full of vivid detail. | candid | candle :: A "candle" gives light. | candied :: "Candied" means coated with sugar. | cancelled :: "Cancelled" means called off. | "Candid" means frank and honest.
fb | T | The coastal path winds ___ the cliffs for several miles before descending to a sheltered bay. | along | across :: "Across the cliffs" suggests crossing from one side to the other. | among :: "Among the cliffs" is unusual. | beside :: "Beside" gives only a location. | "Along" suggests following the line of the cliffs.
fb | T | The hotel offers a ___ range of facilities, including a spa, a gym and three restaurants. | comprehensive | comprehensible :: "Comprehensible" means understandable. | comprehending :: "Comprehending" is a participle. | compulsive :: "Compulsive" describes an irresistible urge. | "Comprehensive" means including everything.
fb | T | We were ___ to discover that the museum was closed on Mondays. | dismayed | dismissed :: "Dismissed" means sent away. | dismantled :: "Dismantled" means taken apart. | dismal :: "Dismal" means gloomy. | "Dismayed" describes how we felt.
fb | T | Travellers are advised to ___ for travel insurance well before departure. | arrange | arraign :: "Arraign" means to charge in court. | arrest :: "Arrest" means to seize. | array :: "Array" means to display. | "Arrange" means to organise.
fb | T | The ancient ruins are ___ visible from the road, but a closer inspection requires a short climb. | barely | bare :: "Bare" is an adjective. | barren :: "Barren" means unproductive. | barrel :: A "barrel" is a container. | "Barely visible" means only just visible.
fb | T | The coach driver ___ us that we had only twenty minutes to explore the village. | reminded | remembered :: "Remembered" does not take a personal object like this. | recalled :: "Recalled us" is ungrammatical. | repeated :: "Repeated us" is ungrammatical. | "Remind someone that" gives a reminder.
fb | W | The consultant was asked to ___ the likely impact of the changes on staff morale. | assess | assert :: "Assert" means to state firmly. | assure :: "Assure" means to promise. | assign :: "Assign" means to allocate. | "Assess" means to evaluate.
fb | W | The two firms signed a ___ agreement to share research findings. | reciprocal | reciprocate :: "Reciprocate" is a verb. | recipe :: A "recipe" is cooking instructions. | recital :: A "recital" is a performance. | "Reciprocal" means mutual.
fb | W | The union has threatened to ___ a strike unless the pay offer is improved. | call | make :: "Make a strike" is not the collocation. | do :: "Do a strike" is not the collocation. | take :: "Take a strike" is not the collocation. | "Call a strike" is the standard collocation.
fb | W | Employees are required to ___ with the company's health and safety policy. | comply | compete :: "Compete with" means to rival. | complain :: "Complain with" is not standard. | compare :: "Compare with" means to examine similarities. | "Comply with" means to obey.
fb | W | The merger created a ___ organisation with more than ten thousand employees. | sprawling | sprinting :: "Sprinting" means running fast. | spraying :: "Spraying" means scattering liquid. | spreading :: "Spreading" is more neutral. | "Sprawling" means large and spread out.
fb | S | Inequality has ___ over the past decade, with the richest tenth now owning most of the wealth. | widened | wedged :: "Wedged" means jammed. | wielded :: "Wielded" means used a weapon. | winded :: "Winded" means out of breath. | "Widen" means to become wider.
fb | S | The decline in the bee population has serious ___ for agriculture. | implications | applications :: "Applications" are requests or uses. | explications :: "Explications" are explanations. | complications :: "Complications" are difficulties. | "Implications" are possible effects.
fb | S | The charity relies on a network of ___ who give their time free of charge. | volunteers | volunteered :: "Volunteered" is a verb form. | voluntary :: "Voluntary" is an adjective. | volunteering :: "Volunteering" is a participle. | "Volunteers" are people who give their time.
vf | E | I wish I ___ more attention to my teachers when I was at school. | had paid | paid :: A wish about the past needs the past perfect. | would pay :: "Would" does not express past regret. | have paid :: "Have paid" is not the past perfect. | "Wish" + past perfect expresses regret.
vf | E | She would have gone to the party if she ___ invited. | had been | was :: The third conditional needs the past perfect. | were :: This refers to the present. | would be :: "Would" is not used in the if-clause. | The if-clause uses the past perfect passive.
vf | E | Not until much later ___ the full extent of her kindness. | did he realise | he realised :: "Not until" at the start requires inversion. | he did realise :: The word order is not inverted. | had he realised :: The main clause is simple past here. | "Not until" requires inversion with "did".
vf | T | We were just ___ the town when the car broke down. | leaving | to leave :: "Just leaving" shows an action in progress. | left :: A participle needs an auxiliary. | leave :: "Were just leave" is ungrammatical. | "Were just leaving" shows an action about to finish.
vf | T | The hotel, ___ owners had refurbished it recently, was booked solid for the summer. | whose | which :: "Which owners" does not show possession here. | that :: "That" cannot show possession. | of which :: "Of which owners" is awkward. | "Whose" shows possession.
vf | S | If the authorities ___ earlier, the outbreak could have been contained. | had acted | acted :: The third conditional needs the past perfect. | would act :: "Would" is not used in the if-clause. | act :: A present form does not fit. | The if-clause uses the past perfect.
vc | T | The flight ___ (delay) for three hours when the airline announced a further postponement. | had been delayed | was delayed :: The past perfect shows an earlier state. | has been delayed :: The present perfect does not fit. | delayed :: The flight is the receiver. | The past perfect passive shows a state before another past event.
vc | T | We ___ (drive) for hours when we finally spotted a petrol station. | had been driving | were driving :: The past perfect continuous shows duration before the discovery. | drove :: The simple past does not show duration. | have been driving :: The present perfect does not fit. | The past perfect continuous shows duration before a past moment.
vc | S | The new law ___ (come) into force at the beginning of next year. | will come | would come :: "Would" does not fit a real future. | comes :: The sentence expresses a future event with a modal. | came :: The sentence is about the future. | The future simple expresses a planned event.
vc | S | Scientists ___ (warn) for decades that sea levels would rise. | have been warning | warned :: The present perfect continuous shows duration up to now. | warn :: The present simple does not show duration. | are warning :: The present continuous does not show duration. | The present perfect continuous links duration to now.
es | E | She was deeply [touching] by the news. | touched | "Touched" describes how she felt.
es | E | They have been friends [since] twenty years. | for | "For" is used with a length of time.
es | E | I am looking forward [to] see you again. | seeing | "Look forward to" is followed by -ing.
es | T | We [stayed] in the village for a week when the storm struck. | had stayed | The past perfect shows an earlier period.
es | T | Neither of the hotels [were] available. | was | "Neither" takes a singular verb.
es | W | The report was [wrote] by an independent expert. | written | A passive needs a past participle.
es | S | There has been a sharp rise [on] unemployment. | in | "A rise in" is the fixed collocation.
es | S | The number of cases [are] growing. | is | "The number of" takes a singular verb.
ec | E | I have no objections to him joining the club. | I have no objection to his joining the club. | Formal usage prefers a possessive before a gerund. | Gerund subjects.
ec | E | She suggested me to take a short break. | She suggested that I take a short break. | "Suggest" is not followed by an object and an infinitive. | Verb patterns.
ec | T | The tourist was fined for to drop litter in the park. | The tourist was fined for dropping litter in the park. | After a preposition use -ing. | Gerunds.
ec | T | The ship had been sailing for days when it reached the harbour yesterday. | The ship had been sailing for days when it reached the harbour. | A stray time phrase conflicts with the tense. | Consistency.
ec | W | The staff was informed that the office would be closed for the holiday. | The staff were informed that the office would be closed for the holiday. | In British English the collective noun takes a plural verb when staff are viewed as individuals. | Agreement.
ec | W | She is the manager that I work for her. | She is the manager that I work for. | The pronoun is repeated unnecessarily. | Relative clauses.
ec | S | Climate change is one of the most important issue facing the world. | Climate change is one of the most important issues facing the world. | "One of the" is followed by a plural noun. | Agreement.
ec | S | The government is committed to reduce poverty by half. | The government is committed to reducing poverty by half. | "Committed to" is followed by -ing. | Verb patterns.
tr | E | I would prefer you not to smoke here. | RATHER | I would ___ here. | rather you did not smoke | "Would rather" + past form.
tr | E | Although he was tired, he finished the report. | TIRED | Tired ___, he finished the report. | though he was | A concessive structure.
tr | T | The hotel was within easy reach of the beach. | WALK | The hotel was ___ from the beach. | a short walk | A different structure.
tr | T | I did not know the way, so I took the wrong turning. | KNOWN | If I ___ the way, I would not have taken the wrong turning. | had known | The third conditional.
tr | W | Few employees were aware of the changes. | HARDLY | ___ aware of the changes. | Hardly any employees were | "Hardly any" means almost none.
tr | W | The manager insisted that we should meet the deadline. | MET | The manager insisted that the deadline ___. | be met | The mandative subjunctive.
tr | S | It is not known how many people have been affected. | KNOWN | The number of people affected ___. | is not known | A different structure.
tr | S | Poverty is more widespread than we realise. | ALTHOUGH | We do not realise ___ widespread poverty is. | how | A wh-clause.
