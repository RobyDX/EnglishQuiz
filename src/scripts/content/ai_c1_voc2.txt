# C1 vocabulary batch 2: E, T, W, S
mc | E | Her sudden departure took everyone completely by ___; nobody had suspected she was unhappy. | surprise | storm :: "Take by storm" has a different meaning. | shock :: "Take by shock" is not the idiom. | force :: "Take by force" means to seize. | "Take someone by surprise" means to astonish them.
mc | E | He was in two ___ about whether to accept the job in another city. | minds | thoughts :: "In two thoughts" is not the idiom. | ways :: "In two ways" has a different meaning. | heads :: "In two heads" is not the idiom. | "In two minds" means undecided.
mc | E | The two brothers have never seen eye to ___ on money matters. | eye | face :: "See face to face" is a different idiom. | hand :: "See hand to eye" is not an idiom. | ear :: "See ear to eye" is not an idiom. | "See eye to eye" means to agree.
mc | E | She is not one to ___ the boat, and always avoids confrontation. | rock | row :: "Row the boat" is literal. | sink :: "Sink the boat" is not the idiom. | steer :: "Steer the boat" is not the idiom. | "Rock the boat" means to cause trouble.
mc | E | The news came as a bolt from the ___, since no one had expected the company to close. | blue | sky :: "Bolt from the sky" is not the standard form. | cloud :: "Bolt from the cloud" is not the idiom. | storm :: "Bolt from the storm" is not the idiom. | "A bolt from the blue" means a complete surprise.
mc | E | I'm afraid I'm rather out of ___ with modern music; I prefer the classics. | touch | reach :: "Out of reach" means unattainable. | step :: "Out of step" has a different meaning. | line :: "Out of line" means behaving unacceptably. | "Out of touch with" means no longer familiar with.
mc | E | They live in a sprawling house on the ___ of the city, surrounded by woods. | outskirts | outlines :: "Outlines" are shapes or summaries. | outlets :: "Outlets" are exits or shops. | outcomes :: "Outcomes" are results. | The "outskirts" are the outer parts of a town.
mc | E | Don't ___ your chickens before they hatch; the deal has not been signed yet. | count | hatch :: "Hatch" cannot be repeated. | feed :: "Feed your chickens" is literal. | sell :: "Sell your chickens" is not the proverb. | "Don't count your chickens" warns against premature confidence.
mc | E | He is a bit of a ___ and spends most evenings alone with his books. | recluse | refugee :: A "refugee" flees danger. | recruit :: A "recruit" is a new member. | rebel :: A "rebel" resists authority. | A "recluse" avoids other people.
mc | T | The island is accessible only by a narrow causeway that is ___ at high tide. | submerged | subdued :: "Subdued" means quiet or toned down. | subsided :: "Subsided" means became less. | substituted :: "Substituted" means replaced. | "Submerged" means covered by water.
mc | T | Having been rerouted twice, the passengers eventually arrived at their destination hours behind ___. | schedule | timetable :: "Behind timetable" is possible but "behind schedule" is the fixed phrase. | plan :: "Behind plan" is not standard. | time :: "Behind time" has a different meaning. | "Behind schedule" means later than planned.
mc | T | Tourists are drawn to the region by its ___ scenery and unspoilt beaches. | breathtaking | breathless :: "Breathless" means out of breath. | breathing :: "Breathing" is a participle. | breathed :: "Breathed" is a past tense. | "Breathtaking" means stunningly beautiful.
mc | T | They set off at ___ to avoid the worst of the midday heat. | dawn | daylight :: "Set off at daylight" is not the fixed phrase. | sunshine :: "Set off at sunshine" is not the fixed phrase. | morning :: "At morning" is not standard. | "At dawn" means at first light.
mc | W | The firm's success is largely ___ to the energy and vision of its founder. | attributable | attributed :: "Attributed to" is a participle; the question uses an adjective. | attributive :: "Attributive" is a grammatical term. | attribute :: "Attribute" is a verb or noun. | "Attributable to" means caused by.
mc | W | The union has threatened to ___ industrial action if the dispute is not settled. | take | make :: "Make industrial action" is not the collocation. | do :: "Do industrial action" is not the collocation. | hold :: "Hold industrial action" is not the collocation. | "Take industrial action" is the standard collocation.
mc | W | The new software is intended to ___ the process of recruitment. | streamline | stream :: "Stream" is a noun or verb. | straighten :: "Straighten" means to make straight. | strengthen :: "Strengthen" means to make stronger. | "Streamline" means to make more efficient.
mc | W | The board ___ the proposal on the grounds that it was too risky. | rejected | reflected :: "Reflected" means thought or mirrored. | rejoiced :: "Rejoiced" means celebrated. | resented :: "Resented" means felt bitter about. | "Reject" means to refuse.
mc | S | Poverty is often ___ by a lack of access to education and healthcare. | exacerbated | exasperated :: "Exasperated" means extremely irritated. | exaggerated :: "Exaggerated" means overstated. | exacted :: "Exacted" means demanded. | "Exacerbated" means made worse.
mc | S | The charity works to ___ the plight of refugees fleeing conflict. | alleviate | alienate :: "Alienate" means to make hostile. | allocate :: "Allocate" means to assign. | alleviated :: After "to" use the base form. | "Alleviate" means to reduce suffering.
mc | S | The prime minister has been ___ for failing to keep his promises. | castigated | cascaded :: "Cascaded" means poured down. | cast :: "Cast" is a verb with different meanings. | castrated :: "Castrated" is a physical term. | "Castigated" means severely criticised.
fb | E | They have been inseparable ___ childhood and still spend every holiday together. | since | for :: "For" needs a length of time. | from :: "From" does not combine with the present perfect. | during :: "During" does not fit. | "Since" introduces a starting point.
fb | E | He gave a ___ shrug and said that it made no difference to him. | casual | casualty :: A "casualty" is a victim. | causal :: "Causal" relates to cause. | casually :: "Casually" is an adverb. | "Casual" means relaxed and indifferent.
fb | E | After years of neglect, the garden was completely ___ with weeds. | overgrown | overgrew :: "Overgrew" is a past tense. | overgrowth :: "Overgrowth" is a noun. | overgrowing :: "Overgrowing" is a participle. | "Overgrown" means covered with growth.
fb | E | It was a ___ remark, but it left him feeling deeply hurt. | throwaway | throwing :: "Throwing" is a participle. | thrown away :: "Thrown away" is a participle phrase. | throw out :: "Throw out" is a phrasal verb. | A "throwaway remark" is made casually.
fb | T | The tour guide provided a ___ commentary on the history of the castle. | fascinating | fascinated :: "Fascinated" describes people. | fascinate :: "Fascinate" is a verb. | fascination :: "Fascination" is a noun. | "Fascinating" describes the commentary.
fb | W | Employees are expected to ___ strictly to the company's code of conduct. | adhere | adapt :: "Adapt to" means to adjust. | admit :: "Admit to" means to confess. | adore :: "Adore" is not followed by "to". | "Adhere to" means to follow.
fb | W | The firm has a long-standing ___ to equal opportunities. | commitment | committee :: A "committee" is a group. | commission :: A "commission" is a payment or body. | communication :: "Communication" is a different noun. | "Commitment to" means dedication to.
fb | S | Local residents are determined to ___ the closure of the hospital. | resist | resent :: "Resent" means to feel bitter about. | resign :: "Resign" means to quit. | resolve :: "Resolve" means to settle. | "Resist" means to oppose.
vf | E | I'd rather you ___ anything to him until we have confirmed the details. | didn't say | don't say :: After "would rather" + person use a past form. | won't say :: "Won't" is not used here. | haven't said :: The present perfect does not fit. | A past form expresses a preference about someone else's action.
vf | T | The coach, ___ journey takes about nine hours, leaves at midnight. | whose | which :: "Which journey" does not show possession. | that :: "That" cannot show possession. | of which :: "Of which journey" is awkward. | "Whose" shows possession.
vf | W | She would have been promoted had she ___ the deadline. | not missed | not miss :: The inverted conditional needs a participle. | not missing :: A participle needs an auxiliary. | not have missed :: "Had she not have" is nonstandard. | "Had she not missed" is the correct form.
vc | E | She ___ (cry) for an hour when her sister finally arrived to comfort her. | had been crying | was crying :: The past perfect continuous shows duration before the arrival. | has been crying :: The present perfect does not fit. | cried :: The simple past does not show duration. | The past perfect continuous shows duration before a past event.
vc | W | The company ___ (recruit) over a hundred staff since January. | has recruited | recruited :: "Since January" calls for the present perfect. | recruits :: The present simple does not show change. | is recruiting :: The present continuous does not link to a starting point. | "Since" calls for the present perfect.
es | E | He [suffers] from insomnia since his wife died. | has suffered | "Since" calls for the present perfect.
es | T | The hotel is [situate] on the edge of the lake. | situated | The passive needs a participle.
es | S | The committee will [affect] a thorough review. | effect | "Effect" as a verb means to bring about.
