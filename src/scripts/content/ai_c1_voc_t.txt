# C1 vocabulary: travel, places and leisure
mc | T | The village, nestled in a remote valley, remains largely ___ by mass tourism. | unspoilt | unspoken :: "Unspoken" means not said aloud. | unsound :: "Unsound" means unreliable. | unsettled :: "Unsettled" means disturbed. | "Unspoilt" means not damaged by development.
mc | T | The ancient city is a popular tourist ___ and attracts visitors throughout the year. | hotspot | hotbed :: A "hotbed" is a place where something develops. | hotshot :: A "hotshot" is a talented person. | hothouse :: A "hothouse" is a warm greenhouse. | A "hotspot" is a popular place.
mc | T | After a long and exhausting journey, we finally ___ at our destination just before midnight. | arrived | landed :: "Landed" is used for planes and ships. | reached at :: "Reached at" is ungrammatical. | came :: "Came" needs a preposition. | "Arrive at" is used with a destination.
mc | T | The region is famed for its breathtaking ___, from snow-capped peaks to remote fjords. | scenery | scene :: A "scene" is a single view or event. | sceneries :: "Scenery" is uncountable. | scenic :: "Scenic" is an adjective. | "Scenery" refers to natural views.
mc | T | Visitors are strongly advised to ___ travel insurance before setting off on an expedition. | take out | take in :: "Take in" means to absorb. | take on :: "Take on" means to accept a task. | take off :: "Take off" means to remove. | "Take out" insurance means to buy a policy.
mc | T | The tour operator went ___ of business, leaving hundreds of holidaymakers stranded abroad. | out | off :: "Off of business" is nonstandard. | bust :: "Bust of business" is not the idiom. | down :: "Down of business" is not the idiom. | "Go out of business" means to close permanently.
mc | T | They decided to ___ the main tourist routes and explore the lesser-known villages instead. | steer clear of | steer away :: "Steer away" needs "from". | steer round of :: This is not a fixed phrase. | steer out :: "Steer out" is not standard. | "Steer clear of" means to avoid.
mc | T | The cruise ship ___ at the port for only six hours, which gave passengers little time to explore. | docked | docked up :: "Docked up" is not standard. | dock :: A past form is needed. | docking :: An auxiliary is missing. | "Docked" means moored at a port.
mc | T | The trek is not for the ___ of heart; it involves several days of steep climbing at altitude. | faint | fainted :: "Fainted" is a participle. | feint :: A "feint" is a deceptive movement. | fine :: "Fine" does not fit the idiom. | "Not for the faint-hearted" is a fixed idiom.
mc | T | The airline offered passengers compensation for the ___ caused by the cancellation. | inconvenience | inconvenient :: "Inconvenient" is an adjective. | inconveniently :: "Inconveniently" is an adverb. | convenience :: "Convenience" is the opposite meaning. | "Inconvenience" is the noun.
mc | T | Many travellers prefer to ___ a car rather than rely on infrequent public transport in rural areas. | hire | lend :: "Lend" means to give temporarily. | borrow :: "Borrow" means to take from a person. | employ :: "Employ" is for people. | "Hire" means to rent for a short time.
fb | T | The old fishing port has been ___ into a fashionable resort with boutiques and galleries. | transformed | transferred :: "Transferred" means moved. | transmitted :: "Transmitted" means sent. | translated :: "Translated" refers to language. | "Transformed" means changed completely.
fb | T | Passengers are requested to remain seated until the aircraft has come to a complete ___. | standstill | standpoint :: A "standpoint" is a viewpoint. | standard :: A "standard" is a level of quality. | standby :: "Standby" means waiting to be used. | "Come to a standstill" means to stop completely.
fb | T | The festival draws visitors from far and ___, many of whom book a year in advance. | wide | long :: "Far and long" is not the idiom. | broad :: "Far and broad" is not the idiom. | near :: "Far and near" is not the idiom. | "Far and wide" means from many places.
fb | T | We took a ___ route through the mountains to avoid the traffic on the main road. | circuitous | circular :: "Circular" means round. | circumspect :: "Circumspect" means cautious. | circumstantial :: "Circumstantial" refers to evidence. | "Circuitous" means indirect and longer.
vf | T | The ferry, ___ crossing normally takes forty minutes, was delayed by fog. | whose | which :: "Which crossing" does not show possession. | that :: "That" cannot follow a comma. | of which :: "Of which crossing" is awkward. | "Whose" shows possession.
vf | T | Had we known about the strike, we ___ a different route. | would have chosen | would choose :: This refers to the present. | will choose :: The conditional is unreal. | had chosen :: The result clause needs a modal. | The inverted third conditional uses "would have" + participle.
vf | T | The expedition, which ___ for months, was finally ready to depart. | had been planned | was planned :: The past perfect passive shows an earlier process. | has been planned :: The present perfect does not fit. | planned :: The expedition is the receiver. | The past perfect passive shows a process before departure.
vf | T | Not only ___ the flight delayed, but our luggage also went missing. | was | were :: "The flight" is singular. | did :: "Did the flight delayed" is ungrammatical. | has :: "Has the flight delayed" is ungrammatical. | "Not only" at the start requires inversion.
vf | T | By the time we reach the airport, the check-in desk ___ closed. | will have | would have :: "Would" does not fit a real future. | has :: A present tense does not fit. | is having :: The continuous does not fit. | "By the time" + future perfect.
vc | T | We ___ (drive) for six hours when the engine finally gave up. | had been driving | were driving :: The past perfect continuous shows duration before the failure. | have been driving :: The present perfect does not fit. | drove :: The simple past does not show duration. | The past perfect continuous shows duration before a past moment.
es | T | The hotel is [situate] in the heart of the old town. | situated | The passive needs a participle.
es | T | We [arrived] at the conclusion that the trip was not worth the cost. | came to | The fixed phrase is "came to the conclusion".
es | T | The resort is [renowned] its stunning beaches. | for | "Renowned for" introduces the reason.
ec | T | The tour guide informed us that the museum is closed on Mondays last week. | The tour guide informed us that the museum was closed on Mondays. | Reported speech and a stray time phrase make the sentence inconsistent. | Reported speech.
ec | T | Neither of the hotels were available at such short notice. | Neither of the hotels was available at such short notice. | "Neither" takes a singular verb in careful usage. | Agreement.
ec | T | We were looking forward to visit the temple at dawn. | We were looking forward to visiting the temple at dawn. | "Look forward to" is followed by -ing. | Verb patterns.
tr | T | The resort is famous for its beautiful beaches. | RENOWNED | The resort is ___ beautiful beaches. | renowned for its | "Renowned for" introduces the reason for fame.
tr | T | We did not expect the crowds to be so huge. | SUCH | We did not expect ___ crowds. | such huge | "Such" is followed by an adjective and a noun.
tr | T | It was not until we reached the summit that we saw the view. | ONLY | ___ we saw the view. | Only when we reached the summit did | "Only when" requires inversion.
