# A2 work, education, technology: sentence-choice (typical mistakes), word-order, odd-one-out
sc | W+PREP | I'm good at maths, but I'm not good at history. | I'm good in maths, but I'm not good in history. :: We say "good at" a subject, not "good in". | I'm good on maths, but I'm not good on history. :: "on" is for surfaces and days; for skills we say "good at". | The correct phrase is "good at".
sc | W+PREP | She works for a big computer company. | She works to a big computer company. :: We say "work for" a company. | She works at for a big computer company. :: "at for" is not correct; we say "work for". | We say "work for a company".
sc | W+PREP | He is interested in science and technology. | He is interested on science and technology. :: "on" is not used after "interested"; we say "interested in". | He is interested at science and technology. :: "at" is used with "good at", but with "interested" we say "in". | The correct phrase is "interested in".
sc | W+PREP | My sister is studying for her final exams. | My sister is studying to her final exams. :: To say why she studies, we use "for". | My sister is studying about her final exams. :: "study about" does not fit with exams; we say "study for an exam". | We say "study for an exam".
sc | W+PREP | I'm waiting for an important email. | I'm waiting an important email. :: We say "wait for" something. | I'm waiting to an important email. :: We say "wait for", not "wait to". | The correct phrase is "wait for".
sc | W+VT | I started working here three years ago. | I started working here since three years. :: "since" needs a point in time; for a period before now we use "ago" with the past simple. | I started working here for three years ago. :: "for" and "ago" do not go together. | Use "three years ago" with the past simple.
sc | W+VT | Yesterday I went to the library and borrowed three books. | Yesterday I go to the library and borrowed three books. :: "Yesterday" needs the past simple: went. | Yesterday I went to the library and borrow three books. :: Both verbs must be in the past simple: borrowed. | Use the past simple with "yesterday".
sc | W+VT | Did you finish your homework last night? | Do you finished your homework last night? :: A question in the past uses "Did + base verb". | Did you finished your homework last night? :: After "Did" we use the base form: finish. | In past questions use "Did + base form".
sc | W+VT | She didn't go to work because she was ill. | She didn't went to work because she was ill. :: After "didn't" we use the base form: go. | She not went to work because she was ill. :: The negative needs "didn't + base form". | After "didn't" use the base form.
sc | W+VT | I'm working on a big project at the moment. | I work on a big project at the moment. :: "At the moment" asks for the present continuous: am working. | I'm work on a big project at the moment. :: After "am" we need -ing: working. | Use am/is/are + -ing for now.
sc | W+VT | He is going to start a new job next month. | He is going to starts a new job next month. :: After "going to" we use the base form. | He going to start a new job next month. :: We need "is" before "going to". | Use "is going to + base verb" for plans.
sc | W+MOD | You should study more if you want to pass the exam. | You should to study more if you want to pass the exam. :: After "should" we use the base form without "to". | You should studying more if you want to pass the exam. :: After "should" we use the base form. | After "should" use the base form.
sc | W+MOD | You must wear a uniform at this school. | You must to wear a uniform at this school. :: After "must" we use the base form without "to". | You must wearing a uniform at this school. :: After "must" we use the base form. | After "must" use the base form.
sc | W+MOD | Can I use your phone, please? | Can I to use your phone, please? :: After "can" we use the base form without "to". | Can I using your phone, please? :: After "can" we use the base form. | After "can" use the base form.
sc | W+MOD | I have to get up at six every morning. | I must to get up at six every morning. :: After "must" we use the base form without "to". | I have get up at six every morning. :: "have to" needs "to" before the verb. | "Have to + base form" shows obligation.
sc | W+ART | Can you give me some advice about my CV? | Can you give me an advice about my CV? :: "advice" is uncountable, so we cannot use "an". | Can you give me some advices about my CV? :: "advice" has no plural. | Use "some advice".
sc | W+ART | We have a lot of homework today. | We have a lot of homeworks today. :: "homework" is uncountable. | We have many homework today. :: With uncountable nouns we use "much" or "a lot of", not "many". | Use "a lot of homework".
sc | W+ART | How much money do you earn? | How many money do you earn? :: "money" is uncountable, so we use "much". | How much moneys do you earn? :: "money" has no plural here. | Use "How much" for uncountable nouns.
sc | W+ART | There are twenty students in my class. | There is twenty students in my class. :: "students" is plural, so we use "are". | There are twenty student in my class. :: After a number we need a plural noun: students. | Plural nouns take "are".
sc | W+GER | She enjoys working with children. | She enjoys to work with children. :: After "enjoy" we use the -ing form. | She enjoys work with children. :: After "enjoy" we need -ing: working. | After "enjoy" use -ing.
sc | W+GER | He wants to become a pilot. | He wants becoming a pilot. :: After "want" we use "to + base verb". | He wants become a pilot. :: After "want" we need "to": wants to become. | After "want" use "to + verb".
sc | W+GER | I'm thinking of changing my job. | I'm thinking to change my job. :: After the preposition "of" we use the -ing form. | I'm thinking of change my job. :: After "of" we use -ing: changing. | After a preposition use -ing.
sc | W+GER | She went to the office early to prepare the meeting. | She went to the office early for preparing the meeting. :: To say why, we use "to + base verb". | She went to the office early for prepare the meeting. :: We use "to + base verb" for purpose. | Use "to + verb" for purpose.
sc | W+ADJ | The new boss is more friendly than the old one. | The new boss is friendlyer than the old one. :: The comparative of "friendly" is "friendlier" or "more friendly". | The new boss is more friendlier than the old one. :: Do not use "more" and "-er" together. | "More friendly" and "friendlier" are both correct.
sc | W+ADJ | This exam was easier than the last one. | This exam was more easy than the last one. :: "easy" has two syllables ending in -y: easier. | This exam was easyer than the last one. :: -y changes to -ier: easier. | The comparative of "easy" is "easier".
sc | W+ADJ | It is the best job in the world. | It is the goodest job in the world. :: The superlative of "good" is "best". | It is the most good job in the world. :: "good" has an irregular superlative: best. | "Best" is the superlative of "good".
sc | W+PRON | My teacher gave us some homework. We have to give it to her on Monday. | My teacher gave us some homework. We have to give it to she on Monday. :: After "to" we use the object pronoun: her. | My teacher gave us some homework. We have to give it to hers on Monday. :: "hers" is a possessive pronoun, not an object. | After "to" use "her".
sc | W+PRON | Is that your laptop? No, it's mine. | Is that your laptop? No, it's my. :: "my" needs a noun after it; alone we say "mine". | Is that your laptop? No, it's me. :: "me" is an object pronoun, not a possessive. | "Mine" shows ownership without a noun.
sc | W+CONF | The teacher told us to open our books. | The teacher said us to open our books. :: After "tell" we have a person: told us. "Say" does not take a person like this. | The teacher spoke us to open our books. :: "speak" does not take "us to + verb". | Use "told us to + verb".
sc | W+CONF | I do my homework every evening. | I make my homework every evening. :: We say "do homework", not "make homework". | I take my homework every evening. :: We say "do homework". | The correct phrase is "do homework".
sc | W+CONF | He made a mistake in the report. | He did a mistake in the report. :: "do" is for tasks (do homework); a mistake is something you "make". | He took a mistake in the report. :: "take" is for things like photos or a bus; we "make" a mistake. | The correct phrase is "make a mistake".
sc | W+CONF | I need to borrow a pen from you. | I need to lend a pen from you. :: "Lend" means to give; "borrow" means to take for a time. | I need to rent a pen from you. :: You rent things like cars or flats, not a pen. | "Borrow" means to take something for a time.
