# B2 vc/vf/es for adjectives, articles, prepositions, pronouns
vc | ADJ | This is the ___ (good) coffee I have ever tasted. | best | better :: "Better" compares only two things. | most good :: "Good" has an irregular superlative. | goodest :: "goodest" does not work here, because "Good" has an irregular superlative. | "Best" is the irregular superlative of "good".
vc | ADJ | Her English is much ___ (good) than mine. | better | best :: "Best" is a superlative, but "than" needs a comparative. | gooder :: "Good" has an irregular comparative. | more good :: "more good" does not work here, because "Good" has an irregular comparative. | "Better" is the irregular comparative of "good".
vc | ADJ | The traffic was ___ (bad) than usual this morning. | worse | worst :: "Worst" is a superlative, but "than" needs a comparative. | badder :: "Bad" has an irregular comparative. | more bad :: "more bad" does not work here, because "Bad" has an irregular comparative. | "Worse" is the irregular comparative of "bad".
vc | ADJ | She is the ___ (intelligent) student in the whole school. | most intelligent | more intelligent :: "More" compares only two. | intelligentest :: Long adjectives take "most". | intelligenter :: Long adjectives take "more". | Long adjectives form the superlative with "most".
vc | ADJ | He drove ___ (careful) than usual because of the ice. | more carefully | more careful :: An adverb is needed to modify "drove". | carefuller :: Long adverbs take "more". | most carefully :: A comparative is needed with "than". | The comparative of an adverb of manner takes "more".
vc | ADJ | The film was ___ (not / as / long) I had expected. | not as long as | not so long than :: "Than" does not follow "as". | not longer as :: "As ... as" needs the base form. | not as longer as :: "not as longer as" does not work here, because "As ... as" needs the base form. | "Not as ... as" makes a negative comparison.
vc | ADJ | I was ___ (disappoint) by the quality of the food. | disappointed | disappointing :: "Disappointing" describes the food. | disappoint :: "Disappoint" is a verb. | disappointment :: "Disappointment" is a noun. | "Disappointed" describes how I felt.
vc | ADJ | He answered the question ___ (quick) and without hesitation. | quickly | quick :: An adverb is needed to modify "answered". | quicker :: A comparative is not needed. | quickness :: "Quickness" is a noun. | "Quickly" modifies a verb.
vc | ADJ | The ___ (old) of the three sisters is a lawyer. | eldest | elder :: "Elder" compares two people. | elderest :: This form does not exist. | more old :: "Old" takes -er or -est. | "Eldest" is the superlative used for family members.
vc | ADJ | Prices are ___ (high) in the city than in the countryside. | higher | highest :: A comparative is needed with "than". | more high :: Short adjectives take -er. | highly :: "Highly" is an adverb. | The comparative of "high" is "higher".
vc | ADJ | He plays tennis ___ (good) than I do. | better | best :: "Best" is a superlative. | gooder :: "Good" has an irregular comparative. | more good :: "more good" does not work here, because "Good" has an irregular comparative. | "Better" is the comparative of "well".
vc | ADJ | He's ___ (far) the most generous person I know. | by | for :: "For far" is not a collocation. | to :: "To far" is not a collocation. | at :: "At far" is not a collocation. | "By far" strengthens a superlative.
vc | ART | He plays ___ (the / piano) every evening after dinner. | the piano | piano :: Instruments take "the" after "play". | a piano :: "A piano" refers to one object, not the skill. | pianos :: A plural noun does not fit. | Instruments take "the" after "play".
vc | ART | She is ___ (a / honest) woman, so everybody trusts her. | an honest | a honest :: "Honest" starts with a vowel sound. | the honest :: The article is specific, not general. | honest :: A singular noun needs an article. | "An" is used before a vowel sound.
vc | ART | I would like ___ (some / information) about the course. | some information | an information :: "Information" is uncountable. | informations :: "Information" has no plural. | a information :: "a information" does not work here, because "Information" is uncountable. | "Some" is used with uncountable nouns.
vc | ART | He didn't make ___ (much) progress this term. | much | many :: "Progress" is uncountable. | a few :: "A few" is used with countable nouns. | several :: "Several" is used with countable nouns. | "Much" goes with uncountable nouns in negatives.
vc | ART | We had ___ (such / a) wonderful holiday that we want to go back. | such a | so a :: "So" is not used before "a + noun". | a such :: "Such" comes before "a". | quite a such :: This is not grammatical. | "Such a" is followed by an adjective and a noun.
vc | ART | She gave me ___ (a / little) money, but it wasn't enough. | a little | a few :: "A few" is used with countable nouns. | few :: "Few" is used with countable nouns. | many :: "Many" is used with countable nouns. | "A little" is used with uncountable nouns.
vc | ART | I like ___ (both) of the dresses; I can't choose. | both | each :: "Each of" takes a singular noun after it. | neither :: "Neither" is negative. | either :: "Either" is used for one of two. | "Both of" refers to two items.
vc | PREP | She is very good ___ (at / learn) languages. | at learning | at learn :: After a preposition use -ing. | in learning :: "Good in" is not the usual collocation. | for learning :: "Good for" means beneficial. | "Good at" is followed by -ing.
vc | PREP | He was punished ___ (for / cheat) in the exam. | for cheating | of cheating :: "Punished of" is not correct. | to cheat :: "Punished to" is not correct. | for cheat :: After a preposition use -ing. | "Punished for" is followed by -ing.
vc | PREP | They congratulated her ___ (on / win) the prize. | on winning | for winning :: "Congratulate for" is not standard. | at winning :: "Congratulate at" is not correct. | on win :: After a preposition use -ing. | "Congratulate on" is followed by -ing.
vc | PREP | I'm fed up ___ (with / wait) in queues. | with waiting | of waiting :: "Fed up of" is informal. | to wait :: "Fed up to" is not correct. | with wait :: After a preposition use -ing. | "Fed up with" is followed by -ing.
vc | PREP | He is responsible ___ (for / organise) the event. | for organising | of organising :: "Responsible of" is not correct. | to organise :: "Responsible to" has a different meaning. | for organise :: After a preposition use -ing. | "Responsible for" is followed by -ing.
vc | PREP | She insisted ___ (on / pay) for the meal. | on paying | in paying :: "Insist in" is not correct. | to pay :: "Insist to" is not correct. | on pay :: After a preposition use -ing. | "Insist on" is followed by -ing.
vc | PREP | We are looking forward ___ (to / see) you next week. | to seeing | for seeing :: "Look forward for" is not correct. | to see :: After "look forward to" use -ing. | at seeing :: "Look forward at" is not correct. | "Look forward to" is followed by -ing.
vc | PREP | He apologised ___ (for / lose) my book. | for losing | of losing :: "Apologise of" is not correct. | to lose :: "Apologise to" introduces the person. | for lose :: After a preposition use -ing. | "Apologise for" is followed by -ing.
vc | PREP | The company is interested ___ (in / hire) more students. | in hiring | to hire :: "Interested to" is possible but changes the meaning. | on hiring :: "Interested on" is not correct. | in hire :: After a preposition use -ing. | "Interested in" is followed by -ing.
vc | PREP | They succeeded ___ (in / climb) the mountain. | in climbing | to climb :: "Succeed to" is not correct. | at climbing :: "Succeed at" is less usual with a gerund. | in climb :: After a preposition use -ing. | "Succeed in" is followed by -ing.
vc | PRON | The man ___ (who) car was stolen reported it to the police. | whose | who :: "Who" cannot show possession. | whom :: "Whom" cannot show possession. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | "Whose" shows possession.
vc | PRON | The woman to ___ I spoke was very helpful. | whom | who :: After a preposition, formal English uses "whom". | which :: "Which" refers to things. | whose :: "Whose" shows possession. | "Whom" follows a preposition.
vc | PRON | We should help ___ (we / other) when we can. | each other | ourselves :: "Ourselves" is reflexive, not reciprocal. | one :: "One" does not fit here. | other :: "Other" cannot stand alone. | "Each other" is a reciprocal pronoun.
vc | PRON | I haven't seen ___ (any) of my old friends recently. | any | no :: "Haven't seen no" is a double negative. | none :: "Haven't seen none" is a double negative. | some :: "Some" is not used in negatives like this. | "Any" is used in negative sentences.
vc | PRON | ___ (what) you need is a good rest. | What | That :: "That" cannot begin this clause as a subject. | Which :: "Which" needs a clear antecedent. | Who :: "Who" refers to people. | "What" means "the thing that".
vc | PRON | She has two brothers, both of ___ (who) live abroad. | whom | who :: After "of" the object form is used. | which :: "Which" refers to things. | them :: "Them" is not a relative pronoun. | "Both of whom" joins the clauses.
vc | PRON | I'd like to know the reason ___ (why) she left. | why | what :: "What" does not follow "reason". | who :: "Who" refers to people. | whom :: "Whom" refers to people. | "The reason why" is a standard structure.
vf | ADJ | The ___ you practise, the better you will become. | more | most :: "Most" cannot form a double comparative. | much :: "Much" is not used in this structure. | many :: "Many" is used with countable nouns. | "The more ... the better" is a double comparative.
vf | ADJ | This is by far the ___ book I have ever read. | most boring | more boring :: "More" compares only two. | boringest :: Long adjectives take "most". | boringer :: Long adjectives take "more". | "By far the most" intensifies a superlative.
vf | ADJ | It's ___ cold that I can't feel my fingers. | so | such :: "Such" needs a noun phrase. | too :: "Too" does not take "that". | very :: "Very" cannot be followed by "that". | "So + adjective + that" shows a result.
vf | ADJ | He's not ___ clever as his brother. | as | so as :: "So ... as" is old-fashioned. | than :: "Than" follows a comparative. | like :: "Like" does not fit this structure. | "Not as ... as" is a negative comparison.
vf | ART | She is ___ best player in the team. | the | a :: "A" cannot be used with a superlative. | an :: "An" cannot be used with a superlative. | – :: A superlative needs "the". | A superlative takes "the".
vf | ART | I haven't got ___ time to talk now. | any | many :: "Time" is uncountable. | few :: "Few" is used with countable nouns. | a few :: "A few" is used with countable nouns. | "Any" is used in negative sentences.
vf | ART | There was ___ little sugar left that we couldn't make a cake. | so | such :: "Such" needs a noun phrase. | too :: "Too" does not take "that". | very :: "Very" cannot be followed by "that". | "So little ... that" shows a result.
vf | ART | He lives in ___ small flat near the university. | a | an :: "Small" starts with a consonant sound. | the :: "The" would refer to a specific flat. | – :: A singular noun needs an article. | "A" introduces a singular countable noun.
vf | ART | ___ of my friends live in the same street. | Both | Each :: "Each" takes a singular verb. | Every :: "Every of" is not possible. | Neither :: "Neither" takes a singular verb in formal English. | "Both of" takes a plural verb.
