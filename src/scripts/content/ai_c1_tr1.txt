# C1 transformations (key word)
tr | VT | I last saw her three years ago. | SEEN | I ___ three years. | have not seen her for ;; haven't seen her for | The present perfect with "for".
tr | VT | It is the first time I have ever been so embarrassed. | NEVER | I ___ so embarrassed before. | have never been | The present perfect with "never".
tr | VT | They started building the bridge in 2018 and are still building it. | BEEN | They ___ the bridge since 2018. | have been building | The present perfect continuous shows duration.
tr | VT | She finished her degree and then she found a job. | ONCE | ___ her degree, she found a job. | Once she had finished | "Once" + past perfect shows an earlier event.
tr | VT | I would never have imagined that the task would be so difficult. | IDEA | I ___ the task would be so difficult. | had no idea | A different structure with the same meaning.
tr | VT | They began talking at nine, and at ten they were still talking. | HOUR | By ten o'clock they ___ for an hour. | had been talking | The past perfect continuous shows duration.
tr | VT | As soon as the lecture ended, the students began to leave. | HARDLY | ___ ended when the students began to leave. | Hardly had the lecture | "Hardly had ... when" is the standard inversion.
tr | VT | He did not realise his mistake until much later. | NOT | It ___ later that he realised his mistake. | was not until much | A cleft sentence with "not until".
tr | VT | I'm sure she is waiting for us at the station now. | WILL | She ___ for us at the station now. | will be waiting | The future continuous can express a deduction about the present.
tr | VT | It is two years since I started learning Japanese. | LEARNING | I ___ for two years. | have been learning Japanese | The present perfect continuous shows duration.
tr | MOD | I advise you not to sign the contract. | SHOULDN'T | You ___ the contract. | shouldn't sign | "Shouldn't" gives advice.
tr | MOD | It was not necessary for them to book in advance. | NEEDN'T | They ___ in advance. | needn't have booked | "Needn't have" means it was unnecessary but done.
tr | MOD | I am certain that he did not take the money. | CAN'T | He ___ the money. | can't have taken | "Can't have" is a negative deduction.
tr | MOD | Perhaps the delay was caused by bad weather. | MAY | The delay ___ by bad weather. | may have been caused | "May have been" expresses possibility.
tr | MOD | I strongly advise you to see a specialist. | HAD | You ___ a specialist. | had better see | "Had better" gives strong advice.
tr | MOD | It was wrong of you not to tell me earlier. | SHOULD | You ___ me earlier. | should have told | "Should have" expresses criticism.
tr | MOD | The rules state that visitors may not enter after six. | ALLOWED | Visitors ___ after six. | are not allowed to enter | "Be allowed to" expresses permission.
tr | MOD | I regret not having asked for his advice. | SHOULD | I ___ his advice. | should have asked for | "Should have" expresses regret.
tr | MOD | They are certain to win the competition. | BOUND | They ___ the competition. | are bound to win | "Be bound to" expresses near-certainty.
tr | MOD | There is a possibility that the meeting will be cancelled. | MIGHT | The meeting ___ cancelled. | might be | "Might" expresses possibility.
tr | PASS | People believe that the painting was stolen in 1990. | BELIEVED | The painting ___ in 1990. | is believed to have been stolen | A reporting passive with a perfect infinitive.
tr | PASS | Nobody has told me about the change. | BEEN | I ___ about the change. | have not been told | The present perfect passive.
tr | PASS | They are going to demolish the old school next year. | DEMOLISHED | The old school ___ next year. | is going to be demolished | The passive of "going to".
tr | PASS | Someone has cut down the old oak tree. | BEEN | The old oak tree ___ down. | has been cut | The present perfect passive.
tr | PASS | Her colleagues respect her enormously. | RESPECTED | She ___ her colleagues. | is enormously respected by ;; is greatly respected by | The passive shifts the focus.
tr | PASS | "I haven't done anything wrong," he said. | DENIED | He ___ anything wrong. | denied having done | "Deny" is followed by -ing.
tr | PASS | "You should have checked the figures," she said to him. | CRITICISED | She ___ the figures. | criticised him for not checking | "Criticise someone for" is followed by -ing.
tr | PASS | The police suspect that he stole the jewels. | SUSPECTED | He ___ the jewels. | is suspected of having stolen | "Suspect of" is followed by -ing.
tr | COND | I did not know the answer, so I did not speak. | HAD | If I ___ the answer, I would have spoken. | had known | The third conditional.
tr | COND | We lost because we did not practise enough. | PRACTISED | If we ___ more, we would not have lost. | had practised | The third conditional.
tr | COND | Without your support, the project would have failed. | HAD | If ___ your support, the project would have failed. | it had not been for | "If it had not been for" means without.
tr | COND | You can borrow the book on condition that you return it. | PROVIDED | You can borrow the book ___ it. | provided you return | "Provided" introduces a condition.
tr | COND | I'm sorry I did not tell her the truth. | WISH | I ___ her the truth. | wish I had told | "Wish" + past perfect.
tr | COND | Would you mind if I opened the window? | OBJECTION | Do you have ___ the window? | any objection to my opening | A different structure with the same meaning.
tr | COND | If you need further help, please contact us. | SHOULD | ___ further help, please contact us. | Should you need | "Should you" is a formal conditional.
tr | COND | If the weather had been better, we would have gone out. | HAD | ___ better, we would have gone out. | Had the weather been | The inverted third conditional.
tr | GER | He found it hard to accept her criticism. | DIFFICULTY | He ___ her criticism. | had difficulty accepting | "Difficulty" is followed by -ing.
tr | GER | It is useless to complain now. | USE | There ___ now. | is no use complaining | "No use" is followed by -ing.
tr | GER | She denied that she had broken the window. | HAVING | She denied ___ the window. | having broken | "Deny" is followed by -ing.
tr | GER | I prefer cycling to driving to work. | RATHER | I ___ to work than drive. | would rather cycle | "Would rather" + base form.
tr | GER | They hope to finish the project by June. | HOPING | They ___ the project by June. | are hoping to finish | "Hope" is followed by an infinitive.
tr | GER | He insisted that he should pay for the meal. | PAYING | He insisted ___ for the meal. | on paying | "Insist on" is followed by -ing.
tr | ART | The number of students who passed was very small. | HARDLY | ___ passed. | Hardly any students | "Hardly any" means almost none.
tr | ART | Both of his parents are teachers. | NEITHER | ___ is not a teacher. | Neither of his parents | "Neither" is negative.
tr | ART | I have not got a single idea. | NOT | I have ___ idea. | not the faintest ;; not a single | A different emphatic negative.
tr | ART | She is very talented, and everyone admires her. | SUCH | She is ___ everyone admires her. | such a talented person that | "Such" is followed by a noun phrase.
tr | ART | It was a great success. | SUCCESS | It was ___. | a great success ;; a tremendous success | The noun phrase.
tr | PRON | The woman is a doctor. I spoke to her. | WHOM | The woman ___ is a doctor. | to whom I spoke | "Whom" follows a preposition.
tr | PRON | The scientists made a discovery. It changed medicine. | WHICH | The scientists made a discovery, ___ medicine. | which changed | "Which" joins the clauses.
tr | PRON | I will take any seat that is free. | WHICHEVER | I will take ___ free. | whichever seat is | "Whichever" means any one of the available.
tr | PRON | The two friends supported each other. | ONE | The two friends supported ___. | one another ;; each other | A reciprocal pronoun.
tr | PREP | The cost is more than we can afford. | BEYOND | The cost is ___. | beyond our means | A fixed phrase.
tr | PREP | I don't agree with the plan. | OPPOSED | I ___ the plan. | am opposed to | "Opposed to" means against.
tr | PREP | They have no intention of changing their minds. | DREAM | They ___ changing their minds. | would not dream of | An emphatic idiom.
tr | PREP | The error was caused by a fault in the software. | DUE | The error ___ a fault in the software. | was due to | "Due to" introduces a cause.
tr | PREP | She resembles her mother. | AFTER | She ___ her mother. | takes after | "Take after" means to resemble.
tr | CONF | He said sorry for his behaviour. | APOLOGISED | He ___ his behaviour. | apologised for | "Apologise for" introduces the reason.
tr | CONF | It is not worth discussing the matter further. | POINT | There ___ the matter further. | is no point in discussing | "No point in" is followed by -ing.
tr | CONF | The firm was unable to pay its debts. | DEFAULT | The firm ___ its debts. | defaulted on | "Default on" means to fail to pay.
tr | PHR | The talks failed because of lack of trust. | BROKE | The talks ___ because of lack of trust. | broke down | "Break down" means to fail.
tr | PHR | We decided to cancel the picnic. | CALLED | We ___ the picnic. | called off | "Call off" means to cancel.
tr | PHR | The idea occurred to her suddenly. | HIT | It suddenly ___ that the plan would not work. | struck her ;; dawned on her | An idiom for sudden realisation.
tr | PHR | She continued to work despite her illness. | CARRIED | She ___ despite her illness. | carried on working | "Carry on" means to continue.
tr | ADJ | There is nothing more important than health. | MOST | Health ___ thing. | is the most important | A superlative.
tr | ADJ | The film was not as good as I had expected. | BETTER | I had expected the film ___. | to be better | A comparative.
tr | ADJ | He is far too young to understand. | OLD | He is not ___ to understand. | old enough | "Enough" follows the adjective.
tr | ADJ | It was a surprisingly easy exam. | EASIER | The exam ___ expected. | was easier than | A comparative with "than".
tr | E | Although he was exhausted, he continued working. | SPITE | He continued working ___ exhausted. | in spite of being | "In spite of" is followed by -ing.
tr | E | She is the kind of person who never gives up. | WOULD | She is not someone who ___ give up. | would ever | "Would ever" expresses character.
tr | E | He only realised how lucky he was when it was too late. | UNTIL | It was ___ how lucky he was. | not until it was too late that he realised | A cleft sentence with "not until".
